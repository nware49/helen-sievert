Bourne Finalizes May Town Meeting Warrants

By Helen Sievert

April 15, 2026

At its meeting on Tuesday , April 14 , the Bourne Select Board approved Annual and Special Town Meeting warrants, with the annual warrant featuring a total of 29 articles and the special warrant featuring three.

Both meetings are slated for Monday, May 4; the Annual Town Meeting will determine spending for the upcoming Fiscal Year 2027, and the Special Town Meeting will allow for adjustments to be made to the current FY26. Tuesday, May 5, has been designated as the second night of Town Meeting, if necessary.

Article 1 is the consent agenda, which includes regular required authorizations, elected officials’ salaries, Chapter 90, revolving funds, and accrued contractual compensated absences. These items “recur annually and are housekeeping in nature,” the draft warrant explained. The Select Board sponsored the article.

Article 2 requests the appropriation of $84,586,115 for the proposed Town of Bourne’s Fiscal Year 2027 operating budget, which is a 2.63% increase from FY26 and includes debt services costs and reflects the proposed adjustment to the FY26 snow and ice budget. The budget, constrained by service demands and shared costs, is now balanced following discussions and adjustments over the past couple of months.

Article 3 requests $1,949,450 for the FY27 Sewer Enterprise Fund. It also requests that “$181,283 be transferred to the General Fund to offset Sewer Enterprise indirect expenses and in order to meet this appropriation, we move that the sum of $2,030,733 be raised from Sewer Enterprise receipts and $100,000 be transferred from Sewer Enterprise retained earnings.”

Article 4 requests $15,380,924 for the FY27 Integrated Solid Waste Management Program (ISWM) Enterprise operating budget, and that “$2,784,942 be transferred to the General Fund to offset ISWM Enterprise indirect expenses, and in order to meet this appropriation, the sum of $18,165,866 is being proposed to be raised from ISWM Enterprise receipts.”

In Article 5 , the Select Board and the Capital Outlay Committee sponsored a number of requests totaling $2,961,350 to cover the costs of capital outlay projects proposed by the Engineering Department, Shore and Harbor Department, Police and Fire departments, Bourne Public Schools, Department of Public Works, Sewer Department and ISWM. Funding sources include free cash, Waterways Improvement Fund, enterprise retained earnings and enterprise borrowing.

Article 6 , which is sponsored by the Select Board, requests that Town Meeting “rescind the vote taken under Article 5-12 of the May 2024 Annual Town Meeting, which authorized the Town to borrow funds for the purpose of the school [wastewater treatment plant] repairs.” The scope of work on the wastewater treatment plant has changed, and different funds are being used, so the previously approved borrowing is no longer needed.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Articles 7 and 8 are related to the relocation of three Algonquin Gas Transmission pipelines, which the Massachusetts Department of Transportation asked Enbridge, the pipelines’ owner, to move due to upcoming construction for the bridge replacement project.

Enbridge is seeking approval to utilize 2.76 acres of town-owned and Conservation Commission-managed land for the project. Because of Article 97, Enbridge is required to provide replacement land for the aforementioned parcels, which the Conservation Committee had approved.

Through Article 7 , Enbridge requests that the Select Board be authorized to petition the state Legislature to file legislation that would allow the Town of Bourne and its Conservation Commission to “transfer, release and convey a 50-foot-wide permanent right-of-way and easement” to Algonquin Gas Transmission, LLC, a subsidiary of Enbridge.

Article 8 seeks to authorize the Select Board and Conservation Commission to transfer the parcels to Enbridge.

Article 9 relates to the betterment and acceptance of Spinnaker Lane and Daniel Webster Lane as public ways; they are currently private ways. The town had received a petition from the homeowners on the roads.

Sponsored by the Select Board, the article would authorize the board to borrow $602,250 for the construction and repair of the roads to meet the town’s regulations. This cost would be paid by tax betterments assessed to the homeowners on the road.

Article 10 , sponsored by the Select Board, will have the town vote to appropriate $1,038,373.70 for resurfacing and temporary repairs of Tara Terrace, Eldridge Street, and Eldridge Street Extension. Unlike the previous article, the roads would remain private and not become public roads. The town had also received a petition from homeowners on the roads. The sum will be repaid through betterments assessed to the homeowners on the roads.

Article 11 , which is sponsored by the Select Board, would allow Town Meeting to hear reports and recommendations from committees and town or state officers.

Articles 12 through 20 are funded through the Community Preservation Act and sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee. The funds can be spent on several types of projects, including affordable housing, historic preservation, and open space/recreation.

The Recreation Department requested $400,000 in Article 12 for the design and construction of the Cataumet Schoolhouse hardcourt project, which would feature a new post-tension concrete court with a 20-year guarantee and fencing to address disrepair, as well as improvements to make the parking, path, and court compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

The Recreation Department requested $350,000 in Article 13 for the Bourne Veterans’ Memorial Community Center shade structures project to provide two 15-foot cantilevered shade structures and ADA-compliant seating and walkways.

The Facilities Department requested $77,0000 in Article 14 to “fund the design costs associated with repairing the exterior envelope of Town Hall, including, but not limited to, design of masonry and window repairs to prevent water infiltration.”

The School Department requested $30,000 in Article 15 for a feasibility study of the central school administration building at 36 Sandwich Road to address siding and trim, which are both nearing the end of their useful lives; the front ramp and stairs, which are cracked; the rusted back entrance staircase; and water penetration at the back entrance.

Article 16 seeks to have the town vote to authorize the Select Board to purchase a conservation restriction for $325,000 for 940 County Road, a vacant wooded lot in Pocasset, as well as $10,000 for “incidental” costs.

Bourne Conservation Trust (BCT), a private nonprofit group, had recently bought the land for $650,000 for conservation purposes and proposed a partnership with the Town of Bourne, under which CPA funds would be used to purchase a conservation restriction on the property, preserving it in perpetuity. Per the CPA, open space parcels must be owned by the town for conservation or recreational purposes or be owned by a nonprofit.

The Housing Assistance Corporation (HAC) requests $100,000 in Article 17 for the renovation of 460 West Main Street in Hyannis to house the Assistance Resource Shelter, Cape Cod’s only state-licensed emergency overnight individual shelter. HAC is seeking $100,000 from every Cape town.

The Bourne Affordable Housing Trust requests $111,500 in Article 18 to support its FY27 budget and affordable housing services. The partnership is solely financed through CPA funds. The funds would assist with purchasing housing, support legal fees, and support continued monitoring by HAC of properties to ensure their affordability, inspections, contributions to home lotteries, grant writing, and Cape Cod Commission assistance.

The Community Preservation Committee requests $75,000 in Article 19 for the CPC’s administrative and operating expenses for Fiscal Year 2027. Requested for the past five years, the funds cover administrative services and costs, such as an appraisal report. Remaining funds go back to Community Preservation Funds.

Article 20 , sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee, requests that the town appropriate $1,931,502.05 from FY27 estimated CPA revenues for the Community Preservation Fund Special Purpose Reserves.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 21 requests $160,000 of free cash be moved into the Stabilization Fund, which Town Administrator Marlene M. McCollem referred to as “the town’s rainy day account.”

Article 22 requests that $850,000 of free cash be moved into the capital stabilization fund and is sponsored by the Select Board. McCollem had previously explained that the article aims to replenish the capital stabilization fund after “over a million and a half” was allocated at the Special Town Meeting in January 2025 to purchase 171 Clay Pond Road for the south side fire station.

Article 23 , sponsored by the Select Board, is a request by the Human Services Committee to transfer $40,000 from free cash to fund the human services grant program for FY27.

Article 24 would have Town Meeting vote to increase the demand fees assessed on unpaid taxes from $15 to $30. Article 25 would increase dog licensing fees from $7 to $10 for spayed female and neutered male dogs and from $10 to $15 for both male and female dogs. Article 26 would increase vital records fees. Birth, death and marriage certificates fees would increase from $10 to $15. And marriage intentions fees would increase from $25 to $40. All three articles are sponsored by the Select Board.

Article 27 , sponsored by the School Committee, requests that $115,962 be raised and appropriated to the Special Education Reserve Fund. This amount was “the savings from the Upper Cape Tech adjustment,” Finance Director Erica Flemming stated previously. “We’re putting that directly towards the Special Education Reserve Fund.”

Article 28 , sponsored by the Planning Board, would amend the Town of Bourne’s accessory dwelling unit (ADU) zoning bylaw to align it with state regulations. The proposed changes include allowing ADUs in downtown neighborhood districts, changing the required number of parking spaces for ADUs and removing lot size and owner-occupancy requirements.

Article 29 , which is also sponsored by the Planning Board, would amend the Town of Bourne’s zoning bylaw to increase the maximum front setback from 15 feet to 30 feet for parcels that are located in the downtown district, but not on a primary street.

The Special Town Meeting was primarily called to provide an opportunity to vote on a budget adjustment for FY26. A citizen’s petition was also included on the special warrant.

Article 1 , which is sponsored by the Select Board, requests $13,334.18 to pay unpaid bills from the Fiscal Year 2025, including a transfer of $11,675.38 from free cash to pay a missed encumbrance of one month’s legal bills, and $1,658.80 from ISWM retained earnings to pay for unpaid ISWM bills.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 2 requests to appropriate $470,000 in free cash to Public Works. This would help cover the $800,000 deficit in the FY26 snow and ice budget.

Article 3 is a petition by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H & J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms, for the town to apply for an annual all alcoholic beverages retail license. The business is located at 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset.

Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram stated that the license Patel is applying for is not available because it has already been granted. He added that the article can be included at the Special Town Meeting, but since the Select Board is the licensing authority, the Town Meeting cannot grant a liquor license.

The applicant applied for a liquor license by filing a citizen’s petition instead of using the standard application process. Since a citizen’s petition can only be withdrawn by the petitioner themselves, Bertram said he would explain the liquor license application process to the petitioner.

Town Meeting will be held on May 4 at 7 PM at the Bourne High School auditorium. More information about both warrants can be found at bourne-ma.gov/1313/Annual-Special-Town-Meeting---May-4-2026 .
