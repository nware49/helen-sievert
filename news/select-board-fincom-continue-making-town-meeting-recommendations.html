Select Board, FinCom Continue Making Town Meeting Recommendations

By Helen Sievert

April 9, 2026

The Finance Committee and the Select Board continued to make recommendations for the remaining articles on Annual and Special Town Meeting warrants, in meetings on Monday , April 6, and Tuesday , April 7, respectively.

Both meetings are slated for Monday, May 4; the Annual Town Meeting will determine spending for the upcoming Fiscal Year 2027, and the Special Town Meeting will allow for adjustments to be made to the current FY26.

Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board must vote on their recommendations for articles prior to the Select Board signing off on both warrants on Tuesday, April 14.

During last week’s review , the Select Board voted to remove articles 24, 25 and 26 from the warrant, which altered the remaining article numbers.

Article 2 , sponsored by the Select Board, requests the appropriation of $84,586,115 for the proposed Town of Bourne’s Fiscal Year 2027 operating budget, which is a 2.63% increase from FY26 and includes debt services costs and reflects the proposed adjustment to the FY26 snow and ice budget. The budget, constrained by service demands and shared costs, is now balanced following discussions and adjustments over the past couple of months. Both the Finance Committee and Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Articles 7 and 8 are related to the relocation of three Algonquin Gas Transmission pipelines, which the Massachusetts Department of Transportation asked Enbridge, the pipelines’ owner, to move due to upcoming construction for the bridges replacement project.

Enbridge is seeking approval to utilize 2.76 acres of town-owned and Conservation Commission-managed land for the project.

Because of Article 97, Enbridge is required to provide replacement land for the aforementioned parcels, which the Conservation Committee had approved.

Through Article 7 , Enbridge requests that the Select Board be authorized to petition the state Legislature to file legislation that would allow the Town of Bourne and its Conservation Commission to “transfer, release and convey a 50-foot-wide permanent right-of-way and easement” to Algonquin Gas Transmission, LLC, a subsidiary of Enbridge.

Article 8 seeks to authorize the Select Board and Conservation Commission to transfer the parcels to Enbridge.

Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem explained that Article 7 is to “allow [Enbridge] to use the protected open space,” and Article 8 is to give “[Enbridge] the right to use the easement.” Both the Finance Committee and Select Board unanimously voted to recommend both of the articles.

Article 9 relates to the betterment and acceptance of Spinnaker Lane and Daniel Webster Lane as public ways; they are currently private ways. The town had received a petition from the homeowners on the roads.

Sponsored by the Select Board, the article would authorize the board to borrow $602,250 for construction and repair purposes to have the roads meet the town’s regulations. This cost would be paid by tax betterments assessed to the homeowners on the road.

Finance Committee chairman James D. Sullivan asked about the article’s benefits to the town. Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem stated that if the roads were taken by the town, “they would count towards the town’s Chapter 90 calculation for aid for public roads.” Both the Finance Committee and Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 10 will have the town vote to appropriate $1,038,373.70 for resurfacing and temporary repairs of Tara Terrace, Eldridge Street, and Eldridge Street Extension. Unlike the previous article, the roads would remain private and not become public roads. The town had also received a petition from homeowners on the roads. The sum will be repaid through betterments assessed to the homeowners on the roads. Both the Finance Committee and Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Articles 16 through 18 are Community Preservation Act (CPA)-funded projects, and are sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee (CPC). The funds can be spent on several types of projects, including affordable housing, historic preservation and open space/recreation.

Article 16 seeks to have the town vote to authorize the Select Board to purchase a conservation restriction for $325,000 for 940 County Road, a vacant wooded lot in Pocasset, as well as $10,000 for “incidental” costs.

Bourne Conservation Trust (BCT), a private nonprofit group, had recently bought the land for $650,000 for conservation purposes and proposed a partnership with the Town of Bourne, under which CPA funds would be used to purchase a conservation restriction on the property, preserving it in perpetuity. Per the CPA, open space parcels must be owned by the town for conservation or recreational purposes or be owned by a nonprofit.

Finance Committee member Wayne Arthur Sampson asked why the town should spend $325,000 for the article. CPC chairman Barry H. Johnson reiterated that the conservation restriction would conserve the land in perpetuity. Sampson asked, since BCT owns the land, “Isn’t it already exempt from any construction on it?” BCT President Jack Dawley said it is not.

Sampson inquired who funded the $650,000 that the BCT used to purchase the property. Dawley said that the BCT assembled funds from partners, including the Compact of Cape Cod Conservation Trusts.

Sampson stated he was “not comfortable with a proposal being made to restrict building on the property when, in essence, the money is going back” to the partners who assisted in funding the purchase. “I think it should have been presented straightforward in that way,” he added. “I think this is a convoluted way to get around it.”

Finance Committee member Thomas Joyce asked who would receive the $325,000. Dawley explained that the funds would be received initially by BCT before being transferred to The Compact of Cape Cod Conservation Trusts, which assists local conservation trusts in acquiring open spaces.

Finance Committee vice chairwoman Amanda Bongiovanni asked if BCT is exempt from paying property taxes. Dawley replied that BCT is a nonprofit and that “the conservation lands are taxed as open space conservation lands.” Sullivan stated that as a non-profit, BCT does not pay any property taxes, to which Dawley said that was his belief.

The Finance Committee voted to recommend the article, with Sampson abstaining.

Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian cited the need to balance developing land for single-family houses with conserving land, the lack of dialogue between the Select Board and BCT, and her disagreement with the use of CPA funds as her reasons for voting no.

“I think that was an opportunity to have a conversation with the trust, but they moved forward and they negotiated a price for that property, and now took out a loan for that property and now want the town to pay off that loan by buying a conservation restriction,” she said. “I just disagree with it.”

Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo noted that the town has experienced difficulty when seeking to purchase open space or a conservation restriction, as this process would require an affirmative vote at a Town Meeting to allocate funds to negotiate for a property. She cited Mashnee Island and Keene House as two instances where the town was unable to secure properties for preservation.

Mastrangelo noted the trust’s interest in the property at 940 County Road stemmed from its proximity to other current or future conservation areas. “I think this is a good purchase for the town,” she added.

Select Board chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti suggested having future conversations regarding the procedure of conserving open space. Mastrangelo proposed a discussion with the Open Space and Community Preservation committees to discuss balancing goals for conserving open space and providing housing. Select Board member Peter J. Meier advocated for “better communication, whenever possible.”

“It is critically important that it be crystal clear which entities are authorized to negotiate on behalf of the town,” McCollem stated. “Because if promises are being made or if people are representing that they’re authorized to negotiate on behalf of the town, that’s very important.”

The Select Board voted to recommend the article, with Siroonian voting no and Meier, Mastrangelo, Select Board clerk Jeanne L. Azarovitz, and Ferretti voting yes.

HAC requests $100,000 in Article 17 for the renovation of 460 West Main Street in Hyannis to house the Assistance Resource Shelter, Cape Cod’s only state-licensed emergency overnight individual shelter. HAC is seeking $100,000 from every Cape town.

As chairwoman of the Bourne Affordable Housing Trust and Housing Partnership, Susan Ross stated that the trust and partnership had voted to support the article due to receiving “calls all the time from people who need shelter…I know for a fact that we need it and that people do use it.”

A majority of Assistant Town Counsel Rian R. Holmquest’s conditions were incorporated into HAC’s application, ranging from a non-discrimination request and a deed rider requiring that the property be used only for a shelter to setting a timeframe for the use of the allocated CPA funds and stipulations about the grant agreement.

In response to one of Holmquest’s conditions about the Town of Barnstable’s involvement with funding, Robert Brennan, HAC’s chief legal officer, stated that HAC was not applying for funding from Barnstable “in light of the significant contributions and burden that they already shoulder as the host community for this shelter.”

Citing unanswered questions and wanting to be on the same page as other Cape towns, finance committee member Sampson proposed to “temporarily hold off on this article.”

The Finance Committee’s vote was undecided, as Bongiovanni and Sampson voted no; Sullivan and clerk Carla Emmons voted yes; and member Thomas Joyce abstained from the vote.

Brennan informed the board on Tuesday that the current location of the shelter at 77 Winter Street was “never designed to be a shelter,” adding that the new location at 460 West Main Street provides more space for programs and is more accessible due to it being on one floor.

The Select Board voted to recommend the article, with Siroonian recusing herself from the discussion and voting to avoid the appearance of conflict; she and her husband own ResCOM Architectural Incorporated, and Housing Assistance Corporation (HAC) is a client.

The Bourne Affordable Housing Trust requests $111,500 in Article 18 to support its Fiscal Year 2027 budget and affordable housing services. The partnership is solely financed through CPA funds. The funds would assist with purchasing housing, support legal fees, support continued monitoring by HAC of properties to ensure their affordability, conduct inspections, contribute to home lotteries, support grant writing and provide assistance to the Cape Cod Commission.

The Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article. The Finance Committee had recommended it last week.

Article 20 requests that the town appropriate $1,931,502.05 from FY27 estimated CPA revenues for the Community Preservation Fund Special Purpose Reserves. The Community Preservation Committee, the article’s sponsor, recommended that $1,503,279.37 would be for the reserve for open space; $214,611.34 would be for the reserve for community housing; and $214,611.34 for the reserve for historic preservation.

Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Article 21 requests $160,000 of free cash be moved into the Stabilization Fund, which McCollem referred to as “the town’s rainy day account.” Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Article 22 requests that $850,000 of free cash be moved into the capital stabilization fund. McCollem explained that the article aims to replenish the capital stabilization fund after “over a million and a half” was allocated at the Special Town Meeting in January 2025 to purchase 171 Clay Pond Road for the south side fire station.

“We can’t do it all in one year, but we’re doing about half of it now at this Town Meeting,” McCollem added regarding replenishing the funds. She noted that it is “important to keep a healthy balance in the capital stabilization fund; this is how we are funding debt service.”

Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Article 27 , sponsored by the School Committee, requests that $115,962 be raised and appropriated to the Special Education Reserve Fund. This amount was “the savings from the Upper Cape Tech adjustment,” Finance Director Erica Flemming stated. “We’re putting that directly towards the Special Education Reserve Fund.”

Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

The Special Town Meeting was primarily called to provide an opportunity to vote on a budget adjustment for FY26. A citizen’s petition was also included on the special warrant.

Article 1 , which is sponsored by the Select Board, requests $13,334.18 to pay unpaid bills from the Fiscal Year 2025, including a transfer of $11,675.38 from free cash to pay a missed encumbrance of one month’s legal bills, and $1,658.80 from Integrated Solid Waste Management (ISWM) retained earnings to pay for unpaid ISWM bills.

Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 2 requests to appropriate $470,000 in free cash to Public Works. This would help cover the $800,000 deficit in the FY26 snow and ice budget.

This article would offset the costs from this past winter by spending down reserves and using the free cash that had funded Articles 24 and 25, which have been deferred to the fall, McCollem said on April 1, adding that the departments would “cover the rest of the deficit with year-end transfers at the end of the fiscal year.”

While the town is allowed to deficit spend in the snow and ice budget and raise the budget on the recap sheet, this would cost the taxpayer.

Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend the article.

Article 3 is a petition by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H & J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms, for the town to apply for an annual all alcoholic beverages retail license. The business is located at 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset. He said he and his cousin own the business and have been there for the past approximately 20 years.

Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram stated that the license for which Patel is applying is not available, since the Select Board had already granted it, “so there’s really nothing [the Select Board] can do.” He added that the article can be included at the Special Town Meeting, but “there’s nothing Town Meeting can do either” as the Select Board is the licensing authority, not Town Meeting.

The applicant applied for a liquor license by filing a citizen’s petition instead of using the standard application process. Since a citizen’s petition can only be withdrawn by the petitioner themselves, Bertram said he would explain the liquor license application process to the petitioner.

Since the Select Board had not discussed the article yet at the time of the Finance Committee’s meeting, the committee deferred making a recommendation until its next meeting. The Select Board decided to take no action and to make a verbal recommendation at Town Meeting.
