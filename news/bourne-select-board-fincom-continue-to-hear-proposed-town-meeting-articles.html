Bourne Select Board, FinCom Continue To Hear Proposed Town Meeting Articles

By Helen Sievert

March 20, 2026

The Finance Committee and the Select Board heard presentations on additional proposed Town Meeting articles this week. The Finance Committee met on Monday , March 16 , and the Select Board met on Tuesday , March 17 .

The committee and the board heard presentations on a pilot health screening program for firefighters, stabilization funds, the consent agenda and reports and recommendations.

Fire Chief David S. Pelonzi, alongside Professional Firefighters of Bourne Local 1717 President and firefighter-paramedic Jared Shaughnessy, presented a proposal for a two-year pilot program to increase accessibility to healthcare for firefighters.

“We don’t have finalized numbers yet,” Pelonzi said. “We’ll bring those forth at a later meeting.”

Through the program the Fire Department would be able to offer “voluntary comprehensive health and cancer screening programs for all employees, including comprehensive physicals, multiple cancer screenings and a medical provider that will come to us and offer essentially one-stop screening for everything,” Pelonzi said, adding that it would be confidential between the patient and the medical provider “unless it comes to the point that someone is deemed unfit...due to some kind of illness.”

After the pilot program is completed, its success will be determined by participant feedback, the presenters said.

The need for the program was recognized during recent union contract negotiations, as Pelonzi noted the increased risk of cardiac arrest and cancers in firefighters. A joint labor-management committee was established, composed of three representatives from the union and three representatives from management, to research and choose a program that would best serve the Bourne Fire Department’s health needs.

Pelonzi noted that most private insurance that the firefighters carry “doesn’t cover a lot of this testing” since “it’s not routine testing that they’d cover and we’d have to pay out of pocket for a lot of it.”

Shaughnessy noted that he himself had a cancer scare last year and “ to have the opportunity to implement a program for early recognition and identifying at-risk individuals is a fantastic opportunity.”

Finance committee chairman James D. Sullivan asked if a screening was to identify something that would require additional examination, would the patient then be referred to their doctor?

“Not necessarily, that’s one option. The other possibility is that they’ll be covered as a 111F claim as essentially an injury on duty,” Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem said. “There would have to be an additional investigation into whatever is found to determine what track it would take from there.”

The Select Board stated its support for the proposal, agreeing that it “sounds wonderful” and “amazing.

McCollem proposed two articles, one for the general stabilization fund and the other for the capital stabilization fund, requesting free cash transfers into the funds.

“We don’t have those numbers yet,” she added. “We’ll present those to you at the next meeting.”

McCollem presented Article 1, the consent agenda. A standard on every Town Meeting warrant, the consent agenda “contains a number of different items that are essentially housekeeping that we need to renew every year in order to make sure that we’ve got all of our proper authorizations,” she said.

In addition to budgeting $100,000 for compensated absences, it also includes “authorizing the limits for revolving funds,” McCollem said.

McCollem also presented Article 11, which would allow reports and recommendations from town committees and officials to be read at Town Meeting.

***

Another round of articles for May Town Meeting was presented before the Select Board on Tuesday, ranging from financial proposals to providing services to the community. The Finance Committee had previously heard these articles on March 9.

As co-chairwoman of the Human Services Committee, Debora Oliviere-Llanes requested that the town approve $40,000 for Fiscal Year 2027.

The committee had received “10 applications for a total amount of $43,600 in funding requests,” Oliviere-Llanes said. “Of the available funds, we were able to allocate $2,000 to print 350 resource guides and the remaining balance of $38,000 was available for grant funding allocation to the human service agencies: AIDS Support of Cape Cod, Alzheimer Family Support, Big Brother Big Sister, Bourne Friends Food Pantry, the Duffy Health Center, Elder Services of Cape Cod, Housing Assistance, Independence House, South Coast Legal and Sight Loss Services.”

On Tuesday, Erica Flemming, the town’s finance director, said that in May 2024, the town had a “borrowing authorization in the amount of $2.1 million for the wastewater treatment plant at the school facility.”

“They have since moved forward with a different repair, and that was voted at the last Town Meeting,” Flemming requested rescinding the original authorization. “We did not borrow the money,” she said.

Flemming presented a request to “increase the demand fee charged for bills subject to such fees,” such as “property taxes, motor vehicle excise, sewer charges, boat excise, mooring and marinas” to $30, which is the maximum amount that it can be increased.

Currently set at $15, a demand fee is “essentially a late fee for nonpayment of taxes,” Flemming said on Tuesday, noting that the increase could “generate double the income or maybe it’ll be an incentive for people to pay on time.” It was previously increased from $5 to $15 after May Special Town Meeting in 2013.

Having identified in last year’s audit report that the town “holds funds that remain unclaimed for extended periods,” mostly uncashed checks, Flemming proposed adopting the general law statute that relates “to the disposition of abandoned funds held by the town.”

“By adopting the statute, we’ll be able to, what they call escheat the funds, and return the funds to the town…as opposed to returning these funds to the state,” she said, referencing findmassmoney.gov .

Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo asked how much money is involved, to which Flemming replied that while she does not have the exact number at the moment, last year’s audit report identified “approximately $77,000 [in] outstanding checks.”

“It’ll go into general fund revenue,” Flemming said. “There is a period that we keep the funds; we have to advertise it, give people the opportunity to collect the funds and once it meets a certain timeline and criteria, then we can return the funds to the town.”

McCollem requested $50,000 in non-capital purchases for the next fiscal year for items that are one-time costs but do not reach the $25,000 threshold for capital equipment purchases.

This is the second time this request has been made. At last year’s May Town Meeting, it was approved, allowing the town to fully purchase a sidewalk snowplow for $17,576.80 and a lawn mower for $12,722.24 to assist with mowing, especially the baseball fields.

The remaining balance of $19,700.96 is planned for a variety of projects. Some $10,000 would be earmarked for concrete repairs to the bleachers at Keith Field to maintain the area while the Recreation Committee continues its work for a more robust project. The remaining balance would be used for an $8,000 project to improve presentation, audio, and visual equipment in the meeting room at the Veterans Memorial Community Center, as well as for a new vacuum cleaner for Town Hall, since the previous one broke.
