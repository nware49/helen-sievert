Bourne Select Board Discusses The County's Proposed Luxury House Transfer Fee

By Helen Sievert and Katie Nelson

January 23, 2026

A draft of a regional home rule petition for a new luxury tax suggests that “luxury homes” be defined as $1 million or more. Previous drafts of the petition proposed that luxury homes be considered $2 million or more.

Under the current draft, individual municipalities may elect to set a higher threshold, which can not be lower than $1 million. The home rule petition will be drafted by the Barnstable County Assembly Delegates and eventually sent to the Massachusetts Legislature for approval.

Prior to the Assembly’s public meeting regarding the matter on Wednesday, January 21, the Select Board heard from A. Wayne Sampson, Bourne’s representative to the Assembly of Delegates, regarding the luxury housing transfer fee during a Select Board meeting on Tuesday , January 20 .

The idea behind the luxury tax is to create more funding for affordable housing. Uncertainties remain on how much control each town will have over those funds. A management board called the Cape Cod Year-Round Housing Fund Management Board would also need to be appointed to manage the county fund created from the transfer fees.

Of the funds collected in the Cape Cod Year-Round Housing Fund, 90% would go to participating towns’ housing trusts. The remaining 10% of the funds would cover administrative costs such as reimbursing costs incurred by the registry of deeds and the Cape Cod regional government administration costs, as well as “supporting regional housing initiatives.”

According to the current draft of the petition, each municipality in the county would be able to designate between 0.5 and 4% of the purchase price of the property interest in excess of the exemption threshold established by the petition, which the assembly proposed should be $1 million.

Focusing on the households in Bourne, Sampson reports that there are 8,900 single-family homes and that the median sales price is $755,000.

“We are very close to that $1 million threshold. Now, if you wanted to do it, you could set that limit at $2 million or $3 million,” Sampson said. “But the question is, is that of any real value to the town, because my understanding is we don’t have a lot of houses in the $2-, $3- or $4-million category.”

Sampson continued to pose questions about the potential effects of the fee on the town, ranging from residents’ ability to sell their homes or having to offset the value of their home, to Bourne potentially having to compete with towns that do not have the fee.

“If you’re going to be adding $10,000, $20,000 or $30,000 to the cost of a home, what is that going to do?” Sampson asked.

While the state Legislature had sought engagement with the local municipalities in the development of the legislature, Sampson expressed concern with the fact that “they have made it very clear that no matter what we have proposed, in order to protect our home communities, they will have final say on what the legislation looks like with no input from us.”

Select Board member Peter J. Meier said that he wanted to meet with the county commissioners to hear their opinions.

“I have a lot of concerns about this…A million dollars is not luxury on the Cape. In some of the towns on the Cape, a million dollars is the median price,” Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo said. “I would go at least two million, probably three million, if it’s really intended to be a luxury tax.”

She continued to state her concern that only 90% of the fee would go to the local communities, with the remaining 10% allocated for administrative fees. Mastrangelo also said that Bourne’s housing infrastructure lacks sufficient staff to handle the transfer fee.

Aware of the need for affordable and workplace housing, Mastrangelo cited the many initiatives in action as alternatives.

“I think maybe it’s a well-intentioned idea,” Mastrangelo said, “But I have too many concerns about how it would affect people trying to sell their house. And when you think of most people, their house is their biggest asset.”

She suggested that if the Town of Bourne were to adopt the legislation, it should be introduced as a ballot question rather than at Town Meeting.

“I wish the other communities that are trying to do this good luck and I appreciate that they are trying to make this a group effort for housing. We definitely need it.” Select Board clerk Jeanne Azarovitz said. However, she agreed with the aforementioned concerns and felt she could not support it as proposed.

Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian echoed the sentiments of the other board members and highlighted her concerns with the potential impacts on the housing market. “Putting a roof over a family’s head is not a luxury,” she said.

“I think it’s going to drive the market up, because someone’s going to lose out,” Siroonian said. “So the seller puts it that much higher, knowing that it’s going to be whatever percentage we decide on as a community or the buyer uses it as a negotiation tool.”

“It’s not the responsibility of someone selling a home or buying a home to solve our housing crisis,” she added.

Another point that Siroonian made was with the influx of initiatives—tax credits for development of affordable housing, MBTA community, zoning changes and the proposed Seasonal Communities designation. She felt that the town has not had an “opportunity to determine whether what we are doing is working.”

She also reiterated Mastrangelo’s point of the existing “avenues and resources” that the town has, such as Community Preservation Act funding and the housing trust.

Referencing her experience as a real estate agent, Select Board chair Melissa A. Ferretti agreed that $1 million is not a lot for a house at the moment and noted that there are many fees already being charged at closing.

While Ferretti said she was “not opposed to finding ways to support our housing,” she felt that she could not support the proposed legislation due to her agreement with the other board members’ statements.

“The county real estate transfer tax is $6.84 per thousand,” Mastrangelo said.

The Select Board unanimously voted not to support the proposed legislation.

The assembly held a public hearing in a hybrid event on Wednesday to gather public input on the potential tax and the million-dollar reduction in defining a luxury home.

The purpose of the meeting was not to debate or discuss the draft home rule petition, Speaker of the Assembly and Yarmouth delegate Susan Warner said. At the meeting, eight individuals from the public spoke up.

The majority of speakers were Barnstable residents; however, Siroonian and Chatham Select Board member Shareen Davis, as well as the Association to Preserve Cape Cod Executive Director Andrew Gottlieb, spoke to the Assembly about their reservations and concerns.

Davis said she feared that Chatham is proposing its own home rule petition with the intention of keeping all funds raised through luxury property transfer fees in Chatham for Chatham housing projects. She said she feared that participation in the county home rule petition might take away the uniqueness of town housing projects.

Gottlieb asked that the draft petition be rewritten to limit housing development in undeveloped land. He requested that the assembly emphasize the importance of minimizing negative environmental outcomes such as increased water quality degradation due to excessive nutrient concentrations, reduced green space and increased economic distortions and traffic implications.

He asked that the committee include language in the petition that prioritizes redevelopment of underutilized existing development. Without the inclusion of those environmental guardrails, Gottlieb said the draft petition has the potential of exacerbating existing problems.

Barnstable residents, including John Julius, Sandra Jones and Eric Schwaab, spoke out against the home rule petition. Schwaab, who is also a commissioner on the Barnstable Housing Authority Board, said lowering the threshold to $1 million homes will negatively impact the town’s housing market. Schwaab asked that representatives from the assembly present their home rule petition to each town’s Select Board in a public meeting.

“Towns should have some say,” Schwaab said.
