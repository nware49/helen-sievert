Residents Press MassDOT On Bridge Replacement Details

By Helen Sievert

April 8, 2026

Residents of the Upper Cape filled the Hermann Foundation Meeting Room at the Falmouth Public Library on Monday as state Senator Dylan A. Fernandes (D-Plymouth and Barnstable) hosted a forum on the Sagamore and Bourne bridge replacement project.

The event, the fourth in a series, gave residents an opportunity to ask questions of Massachusetts Department of Transportation (MassDOT) officials. Topics ranged from funding and traffic management to archaeological procedures and pedestrian and bicycle access.

MassDOT Megaproject Executive Director Luisa Paiewonsky said the agency is still awaiting a response from the U.S. Department of Transportation after submitting a $1 billion funding request last August for the Bourne Bridge replacement. Referencing that uncertainty, Katherine Sturrock of North Falmouth asked about the importance of public engagement during the upcoming election cycle.

Paiewonsky said the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, which owns and operates the bridges, co-proposed the grant with MassDOT. She added that replacing the bridges aligns with federal priorities. “We won’t tell you how to communicate your views on it,” she said, noting that MassDOT has received support from legislators and residents and is continuing to monitor funding opportunities.

Judy Laster of Woods Hole asked what would happen if federal funding is not secured and whether tolls could be considered. Paiewonsky said that without funding, the Army Corps would “have to do, within the next several years, a major rehabilitation of the structure,” but emphasized, “It does not foreclose the option to replace it.”

She added that MassDOT is exploring other funding sources and that Governor Maura T. Healey has said “that there is no contemplation of tolls on the bridge.”

Laster also asked about procedures if archaeological artifacts are discovered during construction. Project manager Anne Canaday said the state’s historic and archaeological commission would be notified and would assist in preserving any findings.

David Anderson of HNTB, MassDOT’s primary consultant for program management and engineering services, said fieldwork on the Cape side of the Sagamore Bridge will begin this month and continue through June. The work will include foundation load tests to evaluate driven piles and drilled shafts, with results shared with contractors bidding on the project.

Meredith Golden of Falmouth, who said she uses the bridges daily, asked what to expect during that fieldwork, especially as it overlaps with repairs the Army Corps plans for the Bourne Bridge in May. Paiewonsky said MassDOT has a traffic management plan for “every piece of work” and noted that the Sagamore fieldwork will not take place in the roadway, though it may be visible or audible from it.

Golden also asked about monitoring for hazardous debris, air pollutants and potential water runoff. Paiewonsky said the state will oversee quality control, inspections and impacts such as dust and noise throughout construction, with help from private partners. While she did not know whether individual wells would be monitored, she said contracts include requirements to limit noise, control dust and prevent runoff.

Steven Sonders of Falmouth questioned why the project is expected to take five years when the current bridges were built between 1933 and 1935. Paiewonsky said, “It is a much different landscape now than it was back in 1933,” noting the bridges were designed for about 1 million cars annually, compared with roughly 38 million today.

She said increased population density and coordination with homeowners and businesses have factored into the timeline, which is intentionally conservative to avoid “let[ting] people down” and to account for potential weather delays.

Fernandes added that the extended timeline also reflects the larger size of the new bridges. Each crossing will effectively consist of “really be two separate bridges right next to each other,” with two wider lanes in each direction, a breakdown lane and a pedestrian and bicycle path.

MassDOT plans to begin construction on the Sagamore Bridge in the winter of 2027–28, with the new southbound span expected to open by 2033. “It will be large enough to move all of the traffic on the existing Sagamore Bridge over onto this span,” Paiewonsky said. “After that, we will demolish the existing Sagamore Bridge, build the northbound span right next to it and then shift all the traffic over.”

One goal of the project is to separate local and regional traffic, a priority many residents have raised, Paiewonsky said.

John York of Cataumet asked for more detail on those plans and advocated for expanded pedestrian and bicycle access. Paiewonsky said specifics will be included in the final environmental impact statement but noted the project includes nine miles of shared-use paths, along with an extension along Sandwich Road toward Gallo Ice Arena and Upper Cape Cod Regional Technical School.

Overall, the full project—including bridge construction, interchange reconstruction, bypass work and landscaping—is expected to take eight to 10 years.

Edward McCormack of Falmouth asked whether a citizens advisory committee would be created to monitor the project, referencing his experience with the Big Dig in Boston. Paiewonsky said MassDOT expects to continue working with the Cape Cod Bridges Advisory Committee, which includes elected officials, civic groups and residents.

She also acknowledged the audience’s reaction to the Big Dig comparison, a project known for cost overruns and delays. MassDOT has studied that effort and similar large-scale projects nationwide to apply lessons learned, she said.

Paiewonsky emphasized that the current estimates are intentionally cautious, supported by independent cost reviews. “We’ve done something called independent cost estimates, where we’ve hired a consultant firm that has no prior history with the project to go through piece by piece, whether our estimates are still good,” she said.

She added that MassDOT will conduct another cost, schedule and risk analysis workshop with the Federal Highway Administration later this month to reassess assumptions and ensure the estimates remain accurate. The agency is also tracking inflation and steel prices.

Fernandes said residents can continue to share feedback through the forum series, advisory committee meetings and his office, which can relay concerns to project stakeholders.

Melissa Murner of Pocasset called for a formal memorandum of understanding among the Town of Bourne, MassDOT and the Army Corps of Engineers, citing Bourne’s role as host community. “People have been trying to urge the selectmen to put it on their agenda,” she said.

Paiewonsky said there is no formal agreement but described ongoing coordination with the town administrator, police department and planner. She noted the town is also involved in reviewing the project’s environmental work.

While acknowledging that construction will affect traffic, Paiewonsky said MassDOT will work “very hard to plan and phase the construction to minimize” disruptions.

She also said there has already been strong interest from contractors and designers following MassDOT’s March 26 request for Letters of Interest for the Sagamore Bridge project. “Teams are already forming,” she said. “There’s a lot of excitement in the industry to bid on this. Good sign for us, because when there’s lots of competition, that keeps the prices lower.”
