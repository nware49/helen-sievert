County Advances Plans For Regional Compost Facility

By Helen Sievert

July 10, 2026

Barnstable County is moving forward with plans to develop a regional composting facility on Joint Base Cape Cod, with officials now working through property transfer, permitting and funding questions for the proposed site.

An update on the Cape Cod Coastal Community Compost Facility was presented by Kari L. Parcell to the Barnstable County Board of Regional Commissioners and the Bourne Select Board on June 29. At the time of the meeting, Parcell was serving as Barnstable County’s waste reduction coordinator. On July 1, she was promoted to Barnstable County’s regional waste program manager for the project.

“My primary goal is to work with towns to divert materials that no longer need to be in a waste stream, to find markets for those materials, to lower cost and to protect our environment at the same time,” she stated.

Parcell has been involved in the development of the composting facility on Joint Base Cape Cod (JBCC) since 2020. The proposed facility would allow JBCC and the four Upper Cape towns’ transfer stations to drop off compost materials. Other stakeholders involved in the project include MassDevelopment and Barnstable County.

Currently, Barnstable County is a “composting desert,” Parcell said, meaning the region lacks the infrastructure needed to process compostable materials such as food and yard waste. Those materials make up “30 to 36% of our waste stream.”

These materials are “easily divertible,” Parcell said, adding that capturing and diverting them “creates protection for our single soil aquifer, with our water and our drinking water. It’s a soil amendment that’s not as nasty as fertilizers.”

Barnstable County paid $150,000 for a feasibility study conducted in 2020 and 2021 to analyze the cost-benefit of the county’s waste management needs. The study recommended examining construction demolition and food waste. The county ultimately determined that developing infrastructure to handle food waste made the most sense through a proposed facility permitted to process 12,000 tons or less of waste annually.

Several meetings were held to identify a potential location for the facility. An available parcel was identified on JBCC, also known as the burrow pit, where the former BMX track was located. The site is adjacent to the Upper Cape Regional Transfer Station, which is also located on JBCC, Parcell said during a July 1 telephone call with the Enterprise.

Parcell said that after discussions with MassDevelopment, which has the right of first refusal for the parcel, the two entities reached a “consensus that we really wanted it, and they understood it and they’re more than willing to work with us, the state, the military in that divestment process.”

In addition to transferring the parcel to Barnstable County, the next steps will include permitting, along with environmental and legal reviews.

After writing a grant request to 11th Hour Racing, an environmental organization based in Newport, Rhode Island, Parcell secured $80,000 to fund engineering services for the project. Following a request for proposal process, the county contracted with SCS Engineers, which drafted a proposed site plan.

The county has also committed another $45,000 to continue advancing the project. The cost would be split among the Upper Cape towns, with each contributing $7,500 after an intermunicipal agreement and memorandum of understanding are completed, Parcell said. KP Law, the county’s legal counsel, is continuing to finalize those documents with JBCC and the Upper Cape towns.

Parcell said she will continue seeking financial assistance for the project through grant requests to 11th Hour Racing and U.S. Congressman William R. Keating (D-MA 9th Congressional District). She also plans to meet with Ben Downing, CEO of Massachusetts Clean Energy Center, later this summer.

Additional discussions will be held with potential operators before the county issues a request for proposals (RFP) to operate the facility.

“The county is going to own or lease the property; we know that much,” Parcell said. “Are we going to be the builders of the facility and own the facility and then hire third-party vendors? That’s still sort of the question.” She added that “it sounds like the county wants to also own the facility.” However, she was “not 100% [sure] on that answer.”

Parcell emphasized the importance of understanding the operators’ perspective before moving forward. If the county were to build the facility and issue an RFP without input from operators, she said, an operator could “come in and say ‘Wait, we have equipment for a 30-yard garage and you only built a 25-yard garage.’”

The site plans prepared by SCS Engineers feature a covered aerated static pile (ASP) system, which is required by both JBCC and the county, Parcell said.

“The facility itself would start with about two active-ish acres of use with about a two-acre buffer,” she said, describing features that would include a receiving building, a woodchip stockpile and a scale for operators to use. “And that can expand because the parcel of land actually goes a little bit more.”

Parcell reiterated that the proposed composting facility would “not be a public place” where residents could drive onto the base to drop off individual compost materials. Instead, JBCC and the towns of Bourne, Falmouth, Mashpee and Sandwich would have their transfer stations deliver those materials to the facility. “This would be an operator pick-up assessment,” she added.

Parcell said the “real hope and dream” is for composting material to be “generated here, processed here and then reused and revitalized in any type of public spaces or town spaces” across Cape Cod.
