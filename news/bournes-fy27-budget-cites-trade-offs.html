Bourne's FY27 Budget Cites Trade-offs

By Helen Sievert

January 8, 2026

Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem presented the proposed Fiscal Year 2027 Operating and Capital Budgets to the Select Board during a joint session with the Finance Committee on Tuesday , January 6 .

The town’s proposed budget for FY27 is estimated at $92,356,851, a 3.57% increase from FY26. McCollem said other Cape towns are seeing growth rates “pushing 10%.”

The operating budget totals $85,330,954. It is a constrained budget that includes “difficult trade-offs.” McCollem said it was balanced without using free cash and constructed on conservative revenue estimates, while aiming to strengthen reserves, maintain “strong community service provision,” and remain in compliance with the new financial policies.

The largest expenditure for 2027 is school expenses at 36.04%, followed by municipal expenses at 28.07%, shared costs at 23.04%, cherry sheet assessments at 6.59%, debt services at 5.88%, reserve fund at 0.27% and overlay reserve at 0.11%. These expenses have increased since last year.

“We’ve budgeted as tightly as we can. There’s no padding left on this budget,” McCollem said. “I have had to find ways to strategically reduce expenses to live within our means.”

To help balance the budget, McCollem recommended defunding the vacant assistant town administrator position. She emphasized that she does not intend to remove the position. McCollem also clarified that she does not recommend across-the-board layoffs, hiring freezes, or leaving vacant positions unfilled.

Citing issues with the systems and the steep price of the program, McCollem also recommended to “delete the funding for the ongoing monitoring and verification costs associated with the recent [Energy Service Company] project.”

As of now, certain revenue assumptions have been made in constructing the next year’s budget. State aid numbers are based on the FY26 budget. The police budget does not account for any wage adjustments for the patrol union while their contract is still being negotiated. Assumptions regarding schools and insurance remain the same as described in the budget preview. Upon receiving current information, updates to these assumptions will be made.

The budget had estimated a 6% increase for the retirement board at the budget preview; however, the town received the preliminary assessment from the Barnstable County Retirement Board, which “came in slightly higher at a 6.64% increase compared to last year,” McCollem said.

In the budget proposal, McCollem had written that “This recommendation is aligned with the strategy to limit borrowing for significant projects and using excess free cash for capital equipment, while addressing critical maintenance and infrastructure needs.”

Next year’s budget estimated that 72% of revenue will be sourced from property taxes.

“The property tax levy reflects an increase in accordance with Proposition 2½, excluded debt, and $475,000 in estimated net growth,” McCollem wrote.

State aid and local receipts are both estimated at 11% of next year’s revenue for the budget. Enterprise offsets follow at 3%, special revenues at 2% and Capital Stabilization for Debt Service at 1%.

New growth is now estimated at $475,000, an increase from the $300,000 mentioned at the December 16 meeting. Estimated increased revenues for FY27 are from motor vehicle excise, license, and permit fees, investment income and energy credits.

“There is new growth that sometimes we are able to capture. And there are other sources of local receipts and state aid that sometimes help offset our budget,” McCollem said. “But those are not guaranteed year to year, so we’re hesitant to push those revenues too hard.”

McCollem drew attention to the fact that the Capitalization Stabilization fund continues to fund the debt services.

“Ideally, we want to bring those costs back inside the budget and fund them under the tax levy,” McCollem said. “We are losing the investment capacity within the operating budget. Hopefully, by the time we are building FY28, we will have reached some sort of agreement on how to change our approach and step back from using capital stabilization as a source so heavily in the operating budget.”

She said next year’s budget is constrained by service demands and shared costs, such as health insurance and retirement, which have grown by more than 4.3%.

“Increasing costs are outstripping our ability to comfortably raise revenue. Our expenses are growing at a higher rate than 2.5%,” said McCollem. “2.5% is what we are comfortably able to rely on generally for property tax increases.”

The only new initiatives in the budget were two promotional opportunities for the Department of Public Works: upgrading two laborers to skilled laborers.

McCollem said that town departments have continued to experience the same needs and are, like last year, requesting additional staffing and programs. However, next year’s budget cannot fund the requests.

“Town departments are feeling a lot of pressure,” said McCollem.

Specifics of FY27 budget’s five-year capital plan will be further discussed at next week’s meeting.

“I do not make these decisions lightly, and I take this responsibility very seriously,” McCollem said. “We’re at the point where we are moving away from talking about the budget in abstract terms, and we are talking about the budget in very concrete terms now, which impacts real people, and it impacts their careers. It impacts their livelihoods. It impacts their families. I want to remain very mindful about that.”

After the presentation was completed, the Select Board members expressed appreciation for the effort that went into preparing next year’s budget proposal.

“I understand the direction where you’re going and appreciate it,” Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo said. “Thank you.”

“I appreciate the fine point to the pencil taken. This was not easy, and there was a lot of discussion,” Select Board clerk Jeanne Azarovitz said. “There was some good news we’ve heard with the rating and so forth. Trudge along.”

On January 13, there will be a discussion on departmental operating budgets and recommended capital projects, including the five-year plan. On January 20, the Sewer Enterprise Fund budget and sewer capital projects will be presented to the sewer commissioners. On January 27, the Select Board will discuss the budget further and make edits before passing it to the Finance Committee for review.
