Bourne Select Board Approves Sale Of BANs Worth $17.2M

By Helen Sievert

January 30, 2026

The Bourne Select Board voted to approve the sale of Bond Anticipation Notes (BANs), totaling to $17,222,239, on January 20. The funds generated from the sale of the bonds will help fund capital projects in town that are either already underway or in the pipeline.

BANs are a “short-term debt instrument used to generate cash for initial project costs and with the expectation that the debt will be replaced later by permanent bonding,” states the Massachusetts Department of Revenue’s Municipal Finance Glossary. Typically sold by town governments as a way of generating money for large capital projects, BANs are “issued for a term of less than one year” and can be “re-issued for up to five years, provided principal repayment begins after two years.”

“Principal payments on school-related BANs may be deferred up to seven years, if the community has an approved project on the Massachusetts School Building Authority priority list,” the glossary stated.

The sale included $3,408,739 of existing BANs and $13,813,500 of new BANs, Finance Director Erica Flemming said.

The funding procured through the sale of new BANs is related to projects that are not currently financed.

The following projects were authorized at the May 2025 Town Meeting: $475,000 for the police gun range; $400,000 for the Jackson Field bleachers; and $1,863,000 for sewer pumps and panels. The following projects were authorized at the October 2025 Town Meeting: $475,000 for the school wastewater treatment plan, $500,500 for fire self-contained breathing apparatus, $2,500,000 for the police station roof and $500,000 for the middle school roof; and $7,100,000 for the Integrated Solid Waste Management building project.

Existing short-term BANs are generally renewed “until the project is complete and we know the final project cost to permanently borrow,” Flemming said. These projects include the following: $779,697 for the construction of the now completed Bourne Intermediate School, which was partially funded by the MSBA, was authorized on October 17, 2016; $487,416 for Buzzards Bay Waste Water Treatment Facility was authorized on October 30, 2017; $79,054 for the Keith Field Lights was authorized on May 7, 2018; $873,969 for the high school roof was authorized on May 6, 2019; $659,229 for a school wastewater treatment plant was authorized on November 16, 2020; and $529,374 for a roof and repairs on fire station number three was authorized on October 21, 2024.

Out of seven bids, Jefferies LLC was awarded the BANs for the net interest cost of 2.3564%.

“This is a reputable firm who has purchased our bonds in the past,” Flemming said. “Rates have come down a bit and, last year, they had a coupon rate of 4% with a net interest cost of 3.1616%.” The town generally awards bonds based on the lowest net interest cost, which reflects the “issuer’s average borrowing cost, net of premiums,” she said.

Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem had prefaced the meeting by stating that the “fire station debt exclusion was not included in this sale…there was a comment made, at maybe last week’s meeting, that the fire station debt has started to show on people’s property tax bills and I just want to be clear—that is not the case. We have not borrowed for the fire station. We’re still in this procedural holding pattern and nothing has hit the tax bills yet.”
