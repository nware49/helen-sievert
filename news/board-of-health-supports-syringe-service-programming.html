Board Of Health Supports Syringe Service Programming

By Helen Sievert

April 23, 2026

The Bourne Board of Health voted to support the syringe service programming (SSP) operated by the non-profit AIDS Support Group of Cape Cod’s (ASGCC) Harm Reduction Outreach Services at its meeting on Wednesday , April 22 .

The program would provide prevention programs, cost- and stigma-free testing, harm reduction strategies and supplies to people who use drugs, safe disposal of syringes and community training and Narcan distribution, said Eliza Morrison, director of the Harm Reduction Outreach Services.

The Town of Bourne, along with many other municipalities, has been receiving opioid abatement funds since 2023 and will continue to do so until 2038 as part of a settlement from a class-action lawsuit against multiple pharmaceutical firms, including Janssen, Teva, Allergan, Walmart, CVS and Walgreens, and distributors. With the funds being legally restricted to be used for harm reduction, treatment, recovery and prevention, the town is set to receive approximately $1,426,486 by 2038.

With the goal of spending the money appropriately and efficiently, the town hired Advocates for Human Potential (AHP), a behavioral health consulting firm, to assist with conducting a needs assessment to identify service gaps in the town’s response to the opioid crisis.

The assessment was composed of 281 full- and part-time residents who participated in the 24 interviews and two focus groups that the Bourne Opioid Settlement Advising Group conducted, with 58% reporting lived experience with the opioid crisis.

A comprehensive plan, developed in June 2025, was developed to guide how the town should spend the abatement funds; the Select Board supported the plan in July 2025. One of the five objectives was to develop a formal partnership with a local syringe service program to bring mobile harm reduction services to Bourne. Kaitlyn Shea, Bourne’s health director, wrote to the Enterprise that a partnership between the Town of Bourne and ASGCC to implement the program in Bourne “is anticipated to be supported through those opioid settlement funds as the plan is carried forward.”

“ASGCC sought the Board’s overall support prior to entering into a formal partnership,” Shea noted regarding Wednesday evening’s meeting. “The Board of Health’s vote last night was not a funding authorization. Rather, it was an important step to support ASGCC’s application for additional funding through the Massachusetts Department of Public Health (DPH), which requires a letter of support from the local Board of Health for SSP operations.”

Morrison noted that data has shown that towns in support of syringe service programs, which are legal in the commonwealth, “have less instances of syringes that are improperly disposed of in the community.”

“There is no data that suggests that any areas that have syringe service programming partnerships have increased violence or crime.” Rather, the data shows “a decline in overdose or substance-related emergency situations,” Morrison stated. She described the syringe service program as “an efficient, effective economic public health measure.”

The program operates from both mobile outreach vans and ASGCC’s Harm Reduction outreach services located in Hyannis, which is the nonprofit’s closest location to Bourne.

The larger van, outfitted with two small offices and a restroom, allows for counseling, testing and linkage of care to be accessible to those who need the support of ASGCC, but cannot make it to one of the organization’s brick-and-mortar locations. “We have quite a few local participants that utilize that service,” Morrison said, noting that the mobile outreach van already provides weekly services to people in Bourne.

Referencing partnerships with churches, outreach organizations and shelters in other areas on the Cape through which the van is able to park for approximately an hour at a time to assist individuals, Morrison inquired that if “anyone in the community of Bourne that is interested in having our mobile unit come and park, we would love to engage.”

Through the syringe service program, the Town of Bourne would also be able to collaborate with ASGCC to organize community training and harm reduction events, ensure that municipal staff have access to Narcan and communicate with other stakeholders to coordinate additional efforts.

ASGCC’s mission statement describes that the organization “saves lives through prevention, education and life-sustaining services that address public health crises to build healthy communities across the Cape and Islands.”

Board clerk Miguel Prieto inquired about how ASGCC connects individuals with services to assist in recovery.

Aside from the syringe program, the ASGCC provides overdose prevention education, Narcan training for anyone and distribution, syringe disposal and retrieval, access to sterile supplies to reduce the risk of infections, free screening, counseling and treatment for HIV, HCV and other sexually transmitted infections, linkage to care in regards to health, a post overdose support team that collaborates with behavioral health and other healthcare organizations, fire and police departments and social workers, and Naloxbox installation, training and refills.

Noting the limited resources on Cape Cod as well as housing, medical and geographic circumstances that make accessing treatment difficult, Morrison said, “We recognize that there is this gray area where people are using substances and where they are accessing recovery services. Our goal is to meet them in the middle so that when they are there, until they are ready for services other than harm reduction services, they are not experiencing overdose.”

She also added that a large facet of ASGCC’s work is done in collaboration with recovery-based organizations, which include outpatient programs and medication-assisted treatment programs, to provide “referrals and connections” for individuals.

Board vice chairwoman Margaret Reali asked about when these services and connections would be offered: “Are these being offered all the time?”

“Every time a participant comes to connect with our programming, we have a comprehensive intake and evaluation,” Morrison replied, explaining that individuals have the opportunity to have a conversation, get tested and/or be referred to a primary care doctor.

In response to board member Donald J. Ellis’ question about the accessibility of Narcan for Bourne residents, Morrison shared that Narcan can currently be found at Upper Cape Cod Regional Technical School and is distributed by other local organizations, and will also soon be installed at various public locations.

The Narcan distribution boxes are monitored in order to ensure that it is properly stocked. Morrison noted, “that it is really useful information to have because if you are looking to fund certain programs addressing substance use, et cetera, you might be able to identify not only locations that are kind of hot spots, but also is this something that we need to be doing more of?”

Board member William “Dusty” Meier noted, “If it is being used, then it is serving a purpose.”

The board expressed its support of the SSP as well as its appreciation for ASGCC’s existing work in the community.

Morrison noted that she is “looking forward to working” with the Bourne Health Department on “how we can connect more fully and really immerse in the community and start providing some more visible support.”
