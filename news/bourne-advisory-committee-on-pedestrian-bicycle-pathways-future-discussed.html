Bourne Advisory Committee On Pedestrian Bicycle Pathway's Future Discussed

By Helen Sievert

February 13, 2026

The scope and charge of the Town Administrator’s Advisory Committee on Pedestrian Bicycle Pathway was discussed by the Select Board on Tuesday , February 10, in light of Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem’s recommendation to dissolve the advisory committee.

Advisory committee interim chairman Kenneth A. Cheitlin proceeded with sharing updates from the committee’s work with the board. A major one is that the committee voted 4-1 to recommend to McCollem a delay in publishing the first phase of the rail with trail project, which would run from Monument Neck Road at John Stackpole Memorial Park to the existing Cape Cod Canal Bike Path. Phase one totalled at $10.2 million.

The committee had been advising the town administrator on the construction of a bike and pedestrian pathway proposed to be built along the Falmouth secondary (F2) rail line as part of a rail-with-trail project.

“Most of [the advisory committee’s] work has obviously been focused on the rail with trail project,” he said. “That project is on hold at this point, so there’s not really anything to be done with that.” Cheitlin said that McCollem had notified the Massachusetts Department of Transportation’s rail and transit division, and that the project has been put on hold.

Cheitlin said that “we are continuing to fight the good fight for the rail-to-trail project,” which is an effort largely led by the nonprofit group Friends of the Bourne Rail Trail. However, “the money that the Friends had secured through the [Cape Cod Regional Transit Authority], following Tom Cahir’s death, that money had been reallocated.”

He continued saying that the state was “prepared to build the rail with trail, hopefully they’ll be prepared to build rail to trail, as well.” Cheitlin also referenced the ongoing effort to have Governor Maura T. Healey greenlight the process and “start the abandonment of the rail line…so that it can be removed.” However, this is a process that will “likely take two or more years,” he said.

The main reason for contacting Healey was that in December 2025, the Falmouth Select Board “voted to change their 2023 position and instead support rail to trail; support the removal of the track.” The board had also written to Healey and Lieutenant Governor Kim Driscoll advocating to replace the F2 and promote the construction of a new alternate track, he said.

“While we’re supportive of the idea, our thrust is obviously to pull up the Falmouth Secondary as a track that’s lightly used and not needed,” Cheitlin said, “the corridor would be better used for the pedestrian bicycle pathway.” He also said that “building the Bourne Rail Trail and extending the Shining Sea Bikeway to the canal would be a reasonable measure to mitigate the damage” from the long-term closure of the Cape Cod Canal pathway when the bridge replacement construction begins.

Cheitlin concluded by saying that while the project “is on hold at this point, so there’s really not much to be done with that,” The Friends of Bourne Rail Trail have continued to advocate for the rail to trail project and have contacted US Representative William R. Keating (D-Massachusetts 9th Congressional District) and state Senator Dylan A. Fernandes (D-Plymouth and Barnstable), who are in favor of the rail to trail project, to contact Healey themselves and “encourage her to at least start the process for abandonment.”

McCollem said that since it is a town administrator’s advisory committee, she felt “responsible” that “the group stays within the bounds of its charge,” being that it is “essentially specific to the Bourne Rail Trail, from extending the Shining Sea [Bikeway] to Bell Road.”

“I think because this immediate project that this scope is specific to has been…stopped for the time being, it makes sense for this committee to be disbanded as a town administrator committee and reconstituted in a new way to advise the Select Board on the broader topics regarding bicycles and pedestrians,” she said. As a town administrator’s advisory committee, its current charge is to implement. “A more town-wide policy or priority-focused committee makes more sense as a Select Board committee,” McCollem said.

The discussion surrounding the proposed dissolution of the Town Administrator’s Advisory Committee on Pedestrian Bicycle Pathway began with Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo, who also served on the advisory committee.

She stated that MassDOT notified the committee in late summer 2025 that, in addition to a roundabout at the end of the bike path, there would be an access point for bikes from the bike path to the Gray Gables neighborhood. These changes increased the project by “$4.3 million at least.”

At a subsequent meeting, some residents supported, and others opposed the changes. Because the deadline to put the project out to bid was at the end of August, the committee voted to delay it, Mastrangelo said. At that time, the town had also not heard back from Healey’s office “whether they were willing to pull the tracks or not.” She also noted that there was a discussion to extend phase one and add phase two, so the project would extend from Gray Gables to Monument Beach.

“My concern is that the Select Board, since July, when this change came, and the additional funding came, and there were changes to the project, the Select Board has never weighed in on what they thought should happen,” Mastrangelo said.

In regard to whom the committee reports to, she said that “it was set up at the time in the charter where if there was going to be a Select Board [member] on the committee,...it had to be a town administrator’s committee. It couldn’t be a Select Board committee, and we’ve changed the charter.”

Mastrangelo said it was established by the Select Board, not the town administrator. “It was for the committee to advise the town administrator so the town administrator could advise the Select Board,” she said. She also stated that she would not feel comfortable with the committee’s dissolution until it had an opportunity to talk with MassDOT about the project.

“I think that dissolving the committee sends a signal that we don’t really care and I think that’s a bad signal to send,” Mastrangelo said. “Does the Select Board want to keep working? Does the Select Board still want there to be a committee that looks into this issue?”

John York, a resident of Cataumet, said he is concerned and interested in “seeing our town keep [its] hat in the ring in terms of bicycle and pedestrian planning of all sorts. I would strongly encourage broadening the charge to make it clear that…this is the committee in our town, when bicycle pedestrian pathway issues come up, it should go to them for their review.”

He also asked, “If you dissolve this committee, what happens to the town’s representation of pedestrian and bicycle interests on the bridges stakeholder advisory group?”

Peter J. Meier proposed delaying the dissolution of the town administrator’s advisory committee until the Select Board has created a new committee and charge in place of it.

“Maybe starting clean would be the way to go to clear up that confusion,” Cheitlin said regarding the differing opinions about what the committee’s charge is and to what entity it reports to.

Select Board acting chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian said that she read the charge as that “it’s specific to the pathway between the Town of Falmouth and the Town of Bourne” and that “all those elements that you’re trying to include” are “all relative to the pathway.” She also said that it is at the discretion of the town administrator to dissolve the committee and is in agreement with Cheitlin that “specific to this charge, the committee work is complete.”

Siroonian suggested that the matter could “become a future agenda item where the Select Board decides to take this under review, broaden the charge and form a committee.”

Select Board clerk Jeanne L. Azarovitz asked, “Is there a way to do it without impacting the continuity of having a representative with the bridges [stakeholder advisory group]?”

Siroonian said that is a question she could ask at the next MassDOT meeting “to see if they’ll still allow representation” or if she and Meier, as the Select Board representatives on the aforementioned group, advocate for bicycle pedestrian pathways.

Mastrangelo asked Siroonian why she believes that the “work of the committee is done as regards to the bicycle pedestrian pathway connection to the Shining Sea [Bikeway]?” Siroonian replied, “Didn’t the committee vote it down to not accept, and didn’t the Select Board also make the recommendation that they didn’t support what…the plan was for the new $10 million plus dollar bill for a quarter mile?”

“I don’t recall that either committee took any votes about it and at the [Metropolitan Planning Organization] meetings that I’ve been going to,” Mastrangelo said, adding that “they basically said we’re waiting to hear from Bourne about what adjustments they want to have based on the proposal that we made for the bike path. We never responded. They made plan changes. We asked them to delay the project.”

“The money for the federal year that ran out in September; we lost that funding,” she said, “but the additional funding that they voted is still there.”

Azarovitz said that at a MPO meeting, Cape Cod Commission Transportation Program Manager Colleen Medeiros presented that the “Falmouth to Bourne portion was done; not active. Another portion was not active. The design money for phase one was still something that was on.”

Mastrangelo then said that when it was stated that a portion is”not active,” it is “because they haven’t funded it because they haven’t reached the milestones in design yet to enable them to get put on the [Transportation Improvement Plan].”

To get on the TIP, “you have to have reached a certain level of design because they want to make sure that you’re going to be at 100% design in five years,” she added. The process to get all of the TIP can take years.

While money and planning have gone into discussing phases three and four, the design process has not reached the point where TIP funding comes into play. However, the funding for phase one is “still on the tip” and “hasn’t been removed because the Town of Bourne has not responded to the design changes that were initiated, where we asked them to delay going out to bid because we weren’t comfortable with the final design.” Mastrangelo said.

She added that “there’s no reason to develop a new charge for a committee if you want to keep doing the work under a different committee and then dissolve. But I think dissolving the committee and saying that we’re not interested in this project is a big mistake for the Town of Bourne.”

As he understood the Open Meeting Law, York said that since “a town administrator’s committee can only have responsibilities that could be responsibilities of the town administrator herself” and “there’s no need to advise the town administrator on policy because the town administrator’s job is not policy,” the Town Administrator’s Advisory Committee on Pedestrian Bicycle Pathway “should be an Advisory Committee to the Select Board because it’s a planning committee and planning is policy.”

Siroonian agreed with York’s assessment. As did McCollem, who said, “The work of this committee is outside of my authority. It should not be a town administrator’s committee.”

“I do not believe that this committee was established under the authority of the town administrator. I believe this committee was voted as a committee by the Select Board and they authorized the town administrator to appoint the membership of the committee,” Mastrangelo said. “If it was established by a vote of the Select Board, I think it has to be dissolved by a vote of the Select Board.”

Meier, who served on the Select Board at the time of the advisory committee’s establishment, said that was his recollection as well, but he would look at the approved minutes from 2018 to verify.

“Until then, we’ll put it on a future agenda,” Siroonian said, thereby concluding the discussion for the time being.
