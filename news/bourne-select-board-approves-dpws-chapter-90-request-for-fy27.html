Bourne Select Board Approves DPW's Chapter 90 Request For FY27

By Helen Sievert

July 17, 2026

Bourne Public Works Director Matthew Sawicki’s request for use of Chapter 90 funds for Fiscal Year 2027 projects was approved unanimously by the Select Board at its meeting on July 7.

The Town of Bourne’s Chapter 90 fund is currently sitting at $4,188,011. Chapter 90, established in 1973, provides reimbursement funding from the commonwealth to municipalities for local transportation needs, including road and bridge repairs and sidewalk improvements.

“In the past four years, we’ve gotten much more money, which has contributed to the decent amount we have right now,” Sawicki said. Regarding Chapter 90 funds, the town received $930,946 for FY27 and $900,000 for FY26. In previous years, the town had received $600,000, he said.

Sawicki added that the town received $313,000 in FY24, $135,000 in FY25 and $252,000 in FY26 from fair-share funding.

Noting Bourne has not paved in a “few years,” Sawicki talked about the Department of Public Works’ (DPW) seven-year outlook, which outlines the town’s proposed paving, bridges, culvert and drainage projects.

Through a Chapter 90-funded collaboration with the pavement management program, the program found that Bourne has “about $12 million worth of work to do to all our town’s roads, just to maintain.” With an average road rating of 72.97, the town has 99 miles of road, Sawicki said.

The program provides estimates and possible repair options for roads. In combination with this, the town uses information obtained from complaints, DPW inspections and input to determine which projects to allocate funding to.

For FY27, Sawicki said, he requested approval to use $3,492,170.28 of the town’s Chapter 90 funds for three projects, which would leave the fund’s balance at $695,840.79.

The first project proposes a two-inch mill and hot mix asphalt overlay of four streets in Cataumet for a total of $2,765,170. The scope of the project would include all of Shore Road except for the section between Tahanto and Barlows Landing Road, since that is the area of the Singing Bridge, which is set to be rebuilt, and Megansett Road, Squeteague Harbor Road, and Red Brook Harbor Road.

“Those are roads in the Cataumet area that are all interconnected,” Sawicki said. “And when we’re paving Shore Road, it makes sense to get those roads while we’re down there.”

He also noted that if this funding request for the project is approved, the aforementioned paving would coincide with the two private road betterment projects done at Spinnaker Lane and Tara Terrace and Eldridge Street, both of which were approved at Annual Town Meeting in May, since the town would “already have the company in place” to do the work.

That work would be completed in the fall, Sawicki said, adding that detours around Shore Road would be provided.

For the second FY27 project, Sawicki requested $297,000 to fund the remaining half of the cost to design and permit the replacement of the Singing Bridge that goes over the Pocasset River. The other half of the capital project cost was approved at Annual Town Meeting this past May.

The third FY27 project would focus on the drainage repair on Washburn Street, for which Sawicki requested $430,000. The project’s design phase had been funded by Chapter 90. This request for additional Chapter 90 funding would go toward completing the construction phase of the project.

“We’re hoping to reduce that $430,000 substantially,” Sawicki said. “There’s some of the work we can do in-house…we’re planning on doing that, but I don’t want to not ask for enough funding in case we don’t have the time or the staff.”

Pivoting to future projects, Sawicki said his hope is to receive grants to fund the Singing Bridge construction in FY28 and FY29; however, if that does not pan out, he said he would pursue using Chapter 90 funds as an alternative.

The DPW would also seek to use rubber chip seal on Herring Pond Road and Head of the Bay Road in FY28 or FY29, he said, noting its cost-effectiveness.

Sawicki said those two roads are good candidates for that paving treatment since “there’s not a huge amount of foot traffic on those roads or bike traffic and that’s where we get the big complaints with a chip seal.” Noting that Plymouth’s portion of Herring Pond Road features chip seal, he said it “made sense to continue it.”

Select Board chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti asked what the complaints entail. “It’s just very rough when it goes down,” Sawicki said. “It goes down as kind of chunks and it gets kicked up.”

“It’s definitely a good tool to have in our case, but we will be very careful to use it,” he added.

Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem noted that “the benefit is that it really does significantly extend the life of the road before you need to mill.”

Sawicki said the DPW hopes to work on repairs to the Back River Bridge and a culvert on Bournedale Road in FY30 and FY31. He added that the department would then like to repave Hilltop Drive, Bluff Road, Clark Road and Norris Road in FY32 and Academy Drive and Maple Street in FY33.

Select Board member Peter J. Meier suggested that the board could file special legislation seeking an appropriation from the state to help cover the cost of repaving Academy Drive, given Massachusetts Maritime Academy’s use of the road.

Sawicki noted that, “When I’m projecting out the funding, I’m not projecting out the fair-share funding in this plan. There have just been so many additions in the past few years, as I pointed out earlier. I’m not comfortable projecting those out.”

He added that there have been internal discussions about when those “fair-share costs come in” and then what the best course of action might be to address the “smaller, local roads and dead-end roads that are in poor shape, because those aren’t going to require some of the additional costs of line painting and drainage work and police details.”

Noting that he is “concerned about estimating too much money ahead in this plan,” Sawicki said the paving management plan would be updated when those “roads come in.”

Select Board clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo noted that at the most recent Pocasset Village Association annual meeting, members had inquired about the design of the Singing Bridge’s replacement.

The design process would include public outreach meetings after the engineers share “what the best design is for that bridge,” Sawicki said, adding that the DPW is “sensitive” to the bridge’s significance in the community.

Mastrangelo noted the importance of prioritizing safety in the replacement bridge’s design, citing a bicyclist’s fatal accident on the span.

Also recalling that accident and since part of Shore Road is designated by the state as bicycle Route 1, John York of Cataumet emphasized the importance of the engineer being cognizant of that fact in order to incorporate an area for a bikeway.
