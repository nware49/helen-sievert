Bourne Select Board Reconfirms Cataumet Fish Liquor License Legislation Language

By Helen Sievert

July 24, 2026

The Bourne Select Board unanimously voted to approve the language featured in the most recent draft of the special legislation liquor license for Cataumet Fish at its meeting on Tuesday , July 21 , reaffirming its vote on June 9.

The bill had originally been introduced in the Massachusetts Legislature as H5084, “an act authorizing the town of Bourne to grant a non-transferable license for the sale of alcoholic beverages not to be drunk on the premises,” the Legislature website states.

However, after some edits were made to the bill. The Legislature, through state Representative David T. Vieira (R-3rd Barnstable), asked the Town of Bourne to provide its approval of the newest and current draft of the bill–H5347, “an act authorizing the town of Bourne to grant an additional license for the sale of alcoholic beverages not to be drunk on the premises,” the Legislature’s website reads.

This process with the Legislature began when Town Meeting members had voted, at the October 2025 Special Town Meeting, in favor of having the Select Board petition the Legislature to issue a “nontransferable, annual, all-alcohol package store license specific to Sea State Foods, LLC, Patrick Ross, Manager, d/b/a Cataumet Fish.” Through its legislative delegation, the town submitted a request for that action.

When the Legislature had sent the bill back with proposed revisions, which were done to standardize special legislation liquor licenses statewide, for the Select Board to review, the board emphasized its intent to reflect the Town Meeting vote regarding the license being nontransferable.

Ultimately, the Select Board unanimously voted to approve the drafted legislation, which allowed Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram to inform the town’s legislative delegation that it shall proceed with ratifying the bill in the Massachusetts Legislature. This occurred after a consensus was reached at a Select Board meeting on June 9 following discussions regarding the language stipulating transferability with Ross, Ross’ attorney Adam Barnosky and Bertram.

The bill would require the state Legislature’s approval before the town can issue it.

In response to Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian seeking to confirm that no additional changes were made other than those previously agreed upon, Select Board clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo said, “It’s a confirmation of what we already voted in.”

The board unanimously voted in the affirmative on a motion made by Mastrangelo, seconded by board member Peter J. Meier, to approve the language of the draft as presented.
