Bourne Library Building Committee Recommends Hiring Pomroy Associates As OPM

By Helen Sievert

May 1, 2026

The Bourne Library Building Committee has taken steps over the course of this past week to hire an owner’s project manager (OPM) to oversee the development of a new town library, culminating in the recommendation to the Select Board its decision to hire Pomroy Associates as OPM.

Six firms—Pomroy Associates, CHA Consulting, P-Three Inc., Accenture, Colliers Engineering & Design and M&J Engineering—had initially responded to the town’s request for proposal (RFP) for an OPM for the project.

Prior to the meeting on Monday, April 27, committee members had individually reviewed the firms’ proposals and utilized a scoring rubric, which is based on the state’s Designer Selection Law, to grade each firm. These scores were then taken by Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem, who created a composite score for each firm.

Pomroy received the highest score, followed by CHA, P-Three, Accenture, Colliers and M&J Engineering.

The committee then reviewed and discussed its rankings on Monday in order to narrow down the candidates it would interview on Thursday , April 30 . “There’s definitely a clear number one; two, three and four are very close together; five and six are a little bit lower,” chairwoman Irja Finn said.

Once the committee voted not to include Colliers and M&J Engineering in further deliberations for interviews, it continued its discussions about the four remaining firms by discussing the firms’ prior similar experience, past performances, financial stability, identity and qualifications of the firm, and other additional criteria.

“Everybody had positives and negatives,” member Elizabeth Kowalski Balaschak said, proposing that since the rankings among the second, third and fourth firms were “very close,” the committee should interview the remaining top four candidates.

Noting a consensus among the committee members’ ranking Pomroy Associates as the first choice, member Carla Emmons inquired about the necessity to conduct further interviews.

Balaschak reiterated that she would like to interview all four firms in order to “have the comparison in person of what they’ve got to offer.”

Finn, vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian and member Tyler Butler agreed with Balaschak’s suggestion.

“I’m in favor of seeing more rather than less people come in because I feel like it can’t hurt having people come,” Butler commented. “Maybe they might change our minds or say something that makes a difference.”

The committee unanimously voted to interview Pomroy, CHA, P-Three and Accenture on Thursday and to interview the firms in reverse order of ranking; member Todd R. Benedict was absent.

The interviews were held on Thursday at the Jonathan Bourne Public Library, with each firm allotted 30 minutes to present, followed by a 15-minute question-and-answer time period, to discuss public outreach, project management, technical aspects, budgets, scheduling and responses to potential emerging issues.

Accenture was first to present before the committee. The project team, based in Boston, explained that although Accenture is a nationwide technological company, it had acquired Boston-based Anser Advisory, formerly known as Pinck & Co. The team itself focuses on OPM and infrastructure and capital projects.

Accenture emphasized the importance of transparency, understanding the nuances of overseeing library construction, early and clear communication with the community and entities involved to achieve the goal. The team also noted the growing role that libraries play in communities.

Committee Clerk Michael McGerigle asked the project team to expand on the commissioning process, to which the team replied that it would begin early in the process and its focus is to ensure that everything is in compliance throughout the project through daily reports. McGerigle also inquired about how the project team would manage various contractors’ opinions and dynamics. The team responded that its role is to maintain the project’s scope and that it remains on track, and that this involves having open and honest conversations with contractors and architects.

Siroonian asked about balancing operational and community needs, to which the project team replied that it uses conservative estimates and accounts for various possibilities when constructing the budget.

Benedict asked the project team how it would increase community engagement. The project team emphasized the importance of understanding the needs, wants, and questions of the community by attending town events or establishing a website.

In response to Balaschak’s inquiry about what characteristics the project team valued in a building committee, the project team emphasized the importance of consensus and clear communication.

Based in Norwell, P-Three specializes in project and construction management, and its founder also has local town government experience. Members of the project team stated the significance of achieving consensus early, open transparency, clear lines of communication, balancing and aligning the interests of all the stakeholders, collaboration, and shared experiences with library construction and procurement, as well as adapting to issues and conflicts.

Committee member Carla Emmons inquired about approaching construction costs. The project team explained that they use formulas to account for potential cost-estimate escalations.

Benedict asked about community outreach. The project team emphasized the importance of informing and reaching out through the mail and organizing face-to-face events at the library, senior center, and community centers.

CHA’s project team, based in Boston, specializes in architecture and public construction projects, with subject-matter experts assisting with other aspects. The project team detailed various experiences with constructing and renovating libraries.

The project team stated the importance of engaging with the community through workshops, community events, digital and print communications, and websites, and maintaining early, clear communication and coordination regarding design, plans, permitting, construction, and quality control. The team also emphasized its implementation of various proactive project management and cost control measures.

Butler inquired about maintaining the schedule, to which the project team stated that the importance of transparency, implementing contingency plans early in the process, and collaborating to resolve potential challenges.

McGerigle asked about CHA’s commissioning process. The project team stated that the process begins in the design phase and involves identifying required products and equipment. He also inquired about CHA’s dynamic with building committees, to which the project team replied that its role is to advise, mediate and collaborate.

Benedict asked about the team’s approach to engaging with the public. The team said that they do so by listening and informing the public by attending and hosting public meetings, sharing the project’s positives, and proposing compromises.

Emmons asked about CHA’s strategy for resolving conflicts, to which the project team stated that, with the goal in mind that every party is successful, it seeks to communicate with all the stakeholders to ensure that what needs to be done is completed.

Pomroy was the last firm to present. Based in East Bridgewater, the firm specializes in project management for municipal buildings. Emphasizing their local presence, the project team shared their various experiences with libraries. The team also emphasized the importance of early, clear, and consistent digital and verbal communication about project goals, needs and costs through face-to-face conversations, forums and public events.

The project team also shared that it provides routine updates to the building committee and emphasized the importance of trust and communication between the committee and the project team. It noted its approach of balancing holding parties accountable with its teamwork dynamic with stakeholders.

The Pomroy team also emphasized the importance of establishing clear expectations, roles, and communication early in the process to ensure alignment. In the event of a schedule delay, the project team stated its approach: identifying the root cause and collaborating with the designer and contractor to implement a mitigation plan.

Regarding the process of commissioning and closing out the project, Pomroy detailed how it uses checklists and action logs to ensure the systems are functioning properly. To control costs, the team highlighted that two independent cost estimates are conducted and a contingency plan is built into the budget.

Benedict asked how the firm would approach resolving conflict, to which the team emphasized the importance of upholding contract stipulations.

McGerigle and Balaschak inquired about Pomroy’s other projects in town. The project team noted that, through its other projects, such as the South Side Fire Station project, it has developed an understanding of the Town of Bourne and sees this as an advantage.

A motion to approve recommending Pomroy Associates as the OPM for the project to the Select Board was unanimously supported by the Library Building Committee.

The committee cited Pomroy Associates’ size as a smaller firm, with the firm’s principal, Richard Pomroy, and partner Taylor MacDonald serving as project managers alongside Alex Murphy, the operations leader and project manager. It also appreciated the project team’s dynamic, chemistry, community engagement and experience.
