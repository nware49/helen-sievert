Bourne Select Board Reviews Liquor License Revisions

By Helen Sievert

March 25, 2026

Discussion surrounding Cataumet Fish receiving a liquor license continued on March 17, as Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram reviewed with the Bourne Select Board the revisions proposed by the state Legislature in connection with Bill H5084.

The bill aims to authorize the Town of Bourne to grant a nontransferable license to sell alcohol for off-premises consumption, the Massachusetts Legislature website states.

The board sought to ensure that the bill properly reflected the vote taken at the October 2025 Special Town Meeting in favor of having the Select Board petition the Legislature to issue a “nontransferable, annual, all-alcohol package store license specific to Sea State Foods, LLC, Patrick Ross, Manager, d/ba/Cataumet Fish.” Through its legislative delegation, the town submitted a request for that action. The Legislature had recently sent the bill back with proposed revisions for the Select Board to review.

Bertram noted that at the Special Town Meeting, it was important that the license be nontransferable, meaning that “while the Select Board would retain its authority to receive the application and do its normal review, the license itself wouldn’t go to anyone else or anywhere else; it would be specific for what we had previously approved.”

The Town of Bourne had an additional liquor license for a package store, which would allow the sale of alcohol for off-premise consumption. These licenses are also known as quota licenses, as they are correlated with the town’s population.

MB Spirits, an affiliate of Market Basket, had originally applied for the license. The Select Board denied that application and “for the last several years, that has been tied up in litigation and that will likely remain the case for the foreseeable future,” Bertram said.

Subsequently, Cataumet Fish, owned and operated by Patrick and Jennifer Ross, applied for the license, which was approved. However, because the Town of Bourne had only one license, Cataumet Fish, “who got approved, is now essentially on administrative hold at the Alcoholic Beverages Control Commission (ABCC) pending outcome of MB Spirits license,” Bertram said. This resulted in the Rosses petitioning for an article at the Special Town Meeting last fall.

“The sense is essentially we approved this for these folks and now they’re sort of in administrative hold; we’ll give them that application,” Bertram said.

Before outlining the impacts of the proposed revisions, Bertram said that “from a lawyer’s perspective, it is actually mostly the same with two differences. One that I think is pretty apparent in the text and the other one is a little more subtle and you need to understand the Liquor Control Act.”

While the revised draft still describes the license as nontransferable from the location, Bertram noted the nuances of one of the Legislature’s proposed edits: “Should the current owner of the business decide to say, sell a business or retire or do something else, they could transfer the license,” he said, adding that edit’s impact could potentially extend the license’s lifespan past the current owner and increase its value as a business asset due to its ability to be transferred.

“If you’re allowing a transfer of owner, it does give the state slightly more control over the license,” Bertram said, noting the difference in the ABCC’s ability to enforce a new license versus the transfer of a license.

The ABCC “cannot order a mobile licensing authority to issue a license,” Bertram said. “They can do a review, they can disapprove of what you said and send it back to you, but they can’t actually order you to issue a new license.”

In contrast, if the current owner were to apply for the license to be transferred, regardless of whether the Select Board decided to deny or approve, “the ABCC can actually tell you what to do,” he said.

Having explained the effects of the state’s proposed revisions, Bertram said, “I’m not necessarily sure that these revisions are consistent with the way we were discussing it on Town Meeting floor.”

“It’s not consistent,” Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo said, as the rest of the board agreed. “That was our question,” Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian added.

If the board were agreeable, Bertram suggested having a conversation with the legislative delegation to explain the town’s position to remain consistent with the affirmative motion made at Town Meeting last fall.

He outlined the two potential outcomes of the conversation. If the Legislature were to accept the board’s decision not to include the revisions, “then all should be well.” If that were not to be the case, “we may not want this to go forward,” Bertram said, adding that an alternative could be to put it before a future Town Meeting to say, “‘Hey, this is a little different than what was previously discussed; Town Meeting, what do you think about this?’”

Bertram reiterated that a conversation is the first step and that he was “previewing” the hypothetical outcomes.

“We should go back to the Legislature and say that the transcript of Town Meeting was clear that it was for this particular situation and particular owner and it is not transferable to a new owner,” Mastrangelo said, noting that the board should also convey to the Legislature that “we understand what you like to do, but it’s not what we discussed at Town Meeting.”

Mastrangelo added that if the Legislature’s response is, “‘Well, that’s a deal breaker,’ we can have a discussion about that.”

Select Board chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti agreed.

Reflecting on the vote at Town Meeting, Siroonian also agreed, emphasizing, “The people’s vote; they’re supporting Cataumet Fish, this license for this individual, not a location.”

In regard to “adding all this language, I disagree,” she said.

Having had the opportunity to discuss the matter with the Select Board, Bertram noted “that the staffers that I’ve talked to over there have been great, so I don’t anticipate this being a hard conversation; whether they decide to change it or not, I don’t know, but I just wanted to put it on the record that they’ve been really great to work with.”
