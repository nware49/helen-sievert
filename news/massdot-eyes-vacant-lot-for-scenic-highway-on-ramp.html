MassDOT Eyes Vacant Lot For Scenic Highway On-Ramp

By Helen Sievert

March 6, 2026

Discussions about the Sagamore Bridge construction continued as Luisa Paiewonsky, Massachusetts Department of Transportation’s (MassDOT) executive director for megaprojects, and David Anderson, lead consultant at HNTB, appeared before the Bourne Select Board on Tuesday , March 3, to provide continued updates on the project’s various facets.

“I thought it would be a good opportunity to give you a quick update on the project itself,” Paiewonsky said. “And then discuss a letter that we’ll be sending to you regarding the former fire station, and then we’d like to continue the discussion about Keith Field parking and give you some perspective on how we see things and hear more from you.”

Paiewonsky informed the Select Board that it will receive a letter regarding the parcel at 1025 Scenic Highway —the location of the demolished former fire station—for the construction of the new Sagamore Bridge.

Since Route 3 and Route 6 are being moved to the west for the new bridge, the on-ramp coming from Scenic Highway onto Sagamore Bridge is being moved to this parcel to ensure designs align with current standards. A bike and pedestrian pathway over the bridge will also be included.

While the ramp and pathway would be located in the northern corner of the parcel, the rest of the property would be needed to construct the improvements, and the property would be needed long-term to perform maintenance on those properties.

The letter “will be requesting, respectfully to the town, that [1025 Scenic Highway] be donated to the Commonwealth in light of its former public use and future public use,” as well as its “public benefits to citizens of Bourne,” she said. “This is a very common request that is routinely granted through municipalities when we need unused municipally owned land for a public purpose.”

Paiewonsky then shifted to present about the mitigation efforts that MassDOT plans to undertake for Keith Field as part of the project to replace the Sagamore Bridge. On January 27 , MassDOT had presented to the Select Board a proposed plan to install a shared-use path along the ballpark’s outfield fence, running from Sandwich Road, across the railroad tracks, to the Canal Service Road.

It also noted that there is currently unauthorized parking on Sandwich Road, which is used by people going to the ballpark. The plan is to install a new authorized parking lot on the opposite side of the road from Keith Field, and a new crosswalk and flashing beacon to provide safe access to the facility.

Paiewonsky stated that as part of the required environmental approval, MassDOT is required to have “agencies with jurisdiction, including the Town of Bourne, the Bourne Recreation Authority and the US Army Corps of Engineers to individually sign off” on “impacts on natural and recreational resources.”

Paiewonsky said that MassDOT has taken a minimal approach, “asserting that the impacts are fairly light and that we are taking all necessary mitigation steps to improve them, to leave everything in a better condition.”

She acknowledged that at the meeting on January 27, the proposed parking lot and crosswalk with a flashing beacon was met with concern about pedestrian safety due to the speed at which people drive on Sandwich Road. Paiewonsky said MassDOT had taken the feedback, however the proposed parcel was “viewed to be the only really viable parcel.”

She noted that despite the fact that the current parking lot for the field is not permitted, meaning that MassDOT is “not obligated to provide substitute parking,” the department would still “like to work out an understanding and get your agreement.”

Wanting to “do it as a good faith gesture for the town in light of all the partnership you’ve shown the project,” Paiewonsky asked, “would you be open to working with us to find the safest way to get people across the street?” She noted that the proposed flashing light beacons are nationally accepted by the Federal Highway Administration.

“I don’t see any harm in trying to find a resolution that is safest.” Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian said. “The biggest concern for the Recreation Committee is finding a safe way to cross…I’m not opposed to listening. I can’t speak for the recreation team.”

As a resident of the nearby neighborhood and clerk of the Recreation Committee, Karen Wilson reiterated the public’s concern that the flashing light will not be enough to stop the road’s fast-paced traffic.

Select Board clerk Jeanne L. Azarovitz agreed that “we should work with you to come up with a solution for a crossing.” She did note that “people do take the path of least resistance, and I think this is going to be the challenge because people in that parking lot are going to want to dash across the street.” Azarovitz also stated that residents of the apartments often cross the street as well.

Paiewonsky said that MassDOT would “like to make it official and provide parking and use our engineering team to work with you and come back with some proposal to get people across safely.”

John York of Cataumet said that at the last meeting on the matter, a Recreation Committee member said that “crossing the road was a non-starter.” Citing the high cost of the project, York said that he “would hope that our town would have the willingness to stand up to this suggestion and put back a counter suggestion that we would prefer parking on the [Keith] Field side,” and “put in the effort now to have the outcome be what everyone prefers.”

In regards to the Town of Bourne donating the parcels to the Commonwealth, Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo asked “what authorization does a Select Board need from Town Meeting in order to do that, “when the decision of Town Meeting is needed” and “what information [Select Board is] going to need to present to Town Meeting in order to have Town Meeting authorize a donation.”

She continued, adding that since “we haven’t ever finalized a policy on giving land or selling land,” the Select Board needs to “understand what authorization we need to pursue this discussion.”

Select Board chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti said that while having parking on the same side of the road as Keith Field may be the ideal situation, “there could be other protections that would happen if we were to negotiate some form of an easier way to get across [Sandwich Road], whether you’re parking or whether you live in Howard Apartments or whether you’re a little kid that lives up at the top of Adams Street or on Cranberry Highway.”

Siroonian asked Paiewonsky to expand on why the proposed parking is the only viable option. Paiewonsky referred the question to David Anderson, the project’s consultant. Prefacing that he is “open-minded to suggestions that people may have,” Anderson said that the “reality is looking at Sandwich Road, all the properties are owned by somebody and are performing some use at the present time.”

“It just so happened that that particular area we’re looking at was in really close proximity to Keith Field and happened to have a portion of the property that didn’t have any use going on,” he said. “That was unique when you look down the corridor, it’s pretty densely populated.” Anderson added that there have “not yet been conversations with the owner of the property that we are looking at; that would be part of the process,” but first wants to ensure “that there is support for something to be there in the first place.”

Recreation Committee member Laurianne Gilbert said she had previously shared suggestions with MassDOT and asked for clarification as to why it is not a viable option for MassDOT to purchase the area east of the baseball field, where a USPS office, Bridgeview Montessori School and a parking lot currently are located.

Anderson said that it “didn’t feel like a feasible thing to do” since parts of that area are either federally owned or already being used as parking, and that MassDOT wants to find additional parking.

He also added that MassDOT had reviewed that area for the shared-use path to the canal path, but faced the aforementioned challenges, as well as ADA-inaccessible terrain. In response to Anderson, Azarovitz noted that the area identified by Gilbert is “chopped up” into various parcels, some of which are owned by the school and a private-land owner, and feature a steep topography.

Paiewonsky said that “we’re looking for solutions that meet the moment in terms of what the need is and not overbuild or overdo it in trying to replace parking.”

“The town should have a little public forum or workshop for the residents to come in and discuss and get ideas,” York said. “This is something that requires that kind of attention.”

Wilson asked, “If you’re requesting the land to be given to the state, that’s permanent, I assume, who’s going to maintain that? Like, if we had the parking lot, is that going to get plowed?”

“If we were to acquire the land, we would be responsible for ownership, maintenance and care,” Paiewonsky said. “The parking lot, I presume, would be taken care of by the town.”

As the design and permitting process of the new Sagamore and Bourne bridges occur simultaneously, Paiewonsky said that a preliminary design will be provided to a contractor upon the completion of the procurement process. She also said that MassDOT will file the final environmental impact report with the Massachusetts Environmental Policy Act Office this week and will eventually be available for public comment.

MassDOT expects to have permits this summer. Upon this and having a request for proposals, a project scope document, MassDOT will have access to $1.2 billion of federal grant funds. Paiewonsky acknowledged people’s concern over ensuring that those monies are secure and said, “We are moving fast to make sure that we can secure those on behalf of the Commonwealth.”

Funding for the new Sagamore Bridge will be composed of the aforementioned as well as US Army Corps of Engineer funding and approximately $750,000,000 from the Commonwealth of Massachusetts.

Paiewonsky also announced that the three-phased procurement process will begin this spring, first with accepting letters of interest from contractors and design teams interested in the project. Forty days thereafter, interested parties will demonstrate their abilities through a request for qualifications.

Paiewonsky proposes that in the fall of 2026 MassDOT will issue a request for proposals and then in the end of 2027 a notice to proceed could potentially be issued to a design and contractor team. Upon receiving this, the chosen team could begin spending for the construction of the new Sagamore Bridge.

“We anticipate that once construction starts, we will start with the mainland to the Cape Cod side of the eastbound bridge,” Paiewonsky said. “When that is completed in roughly 2033,...we’ll shift both lanes, both sides, both directions of traffic over to the new eastbound span, demolish the existing Sagamore Bridge, build the westbound span, and then once that’s completed, split up the traffic and have it running in both directions.”

Paiewonsky stated her appreciation for collaborating with Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem, Bourne Police Chief Brandon M. Esip and Upper Cape Cod Regional Technical School Superintendent Roger Forget. “We cannot push this project forward unless we have this sort of local-state partnership. So, thank you all for that,” she said.

MassDOT will meet at a Cape Cod advisory group meeting on Monday, March 10, and Paiewonsky said MassDOT expects to have continued public comment regarding bridge design in the spring.

Ferretti concluded the meeting stating “we appreciate you coming back in again and hopefully we can have more discussion and maybe a forum or some sort of discussion later. Thank you.”
