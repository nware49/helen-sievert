Bourne Select Board Reviews Special Legislation For Liquor Licenses

By Helen Sievert

June 19, 2026

At its meeting on June 9, the Bourne Select Board aimed to be consistent with the language featured in two individual special legislation liquor licenses for Cataumet Fish and H&J Convenience, Inc.

In regard to Cataumet Fish, Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram provided an update on the status of Bill H5084. It aims to authorize the Town of Bourne to grant a nontransferable license to sell alcohol for off-premises consumption, the Massachusetts Legislature website states.

Town Meeting members voted, at the October 2025 Special Town Meeting, in favor of having the Select Board petition the Legislature to issue a “nontransferable, annual, all-alcohol package store license specific to Sea State Foods, LLC, Patrick Ross, Manager, d/b/a Cataumet Fish.” Through its legislative delegation, the town submitted a request for that action.

The Legislature had sent the bill back with proposed revisions, which were done to standardize special legislation liquor licenses statewide, for the Select Board to review. The board emphasized its intent to reflect the Town Meeting vote regarding the license being nontransferable.

Bertram explained that since its last discussion on the matter on April 10 , there have not been many updates, as the board had wanted to provide the owners of Cataumet Fish, Patrick and Jennifer Ross, an opportunity to speak before the board, before any additional steps were taken.

“We understand that it’s a matter of trying to bring the language into conformity with all of these other acts across the commonwealth that accomplished the same thing,” Patrick Ross said. “But I think, probably the simplest way to put it is that what was presented at Town Meeting was a non-transferable license and what is in draft form in front of the Legislature is also a non-transferable license.”

Adam Barnosky, the Ross’ attorney on the matter, also stated that they believe the draft “sufficiently honors” the Town Meeting vote, and “support it in its current form.”

Addressing the board’s concern regarding the transferability language, he noted that the draft now mentions that the license is specially granted to Sea State Foods at its location at 1360 Route 28A in Cataumet.

“The way I read the text, the board couldn’t accept a transfer application for the license,” Barnosky added.

He noted that to make this license consistent with other special legislation liquor licenses, one of the state’s other edits stipulated that if Sea State were to no longer be in business and somebody else takes over ownership of the market, that new owner would have to return the liquor license to the town.

“Within three years, if someone else at that location applies, the [Select Board] has the option to grant it for that operator,” Barnosky said. “If it’s not acted on within three years, then [the license] sunsets.”

He explained that if this language were not included in the bill, then “if a new operator comes in without the option for applying for a new one, the town will either be without the license or they’ll have to go back to the legislature and kind of go through this whole program again.”

Barnosky noted that the board would still maintain its ability to deny or approve a license in this situation, and it “doesn’t create a commodity, which I think was kind of one of the big issues here.”

Barnosky concluded by highlighting that the Rosses had appeared before the board, which had approved a license for their business “three and a half years ago.”

The Select Board vice chairwoman inquired if Bertram concurred with Barnosky’s interpretation of the nontransferability language. “I concur…transfer or new applicant are kind of terms of art under the liquor control act,” Betram replied, adding that the bill does not feature transferability language, rather it features the process that Barnosky outlined.

However, he did note that “I guess, strictly speaking, it may not in the future, hypothetically be limited to this applicant, but on its face right now, it is and it’s not transferable by them.”

Select Board clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo seconded a motion made by Siroonian to approve the language of the bill as written. The board unanimously voted affirmatively, allowing Bertram to notify the town’s legislative delegation that it shall proceed with ratifying the bill in the state Legislature.

The bill is currently in the joint committee on consumer protection and professional licensure, and would require the state Legislature’s approval before the town can issue it.

The Select Board discussed a request by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H&J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms in Pocasset, for the board to file special legislation for a liquor license.

At the past May Special Town Meeting, a citizen’s petition by Patel was approved by voters . The vote authorized the Select Board to petition the state Legislature for special legislation for an annual all-alcoholic beverages retail license for off-premise consumption at 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset.

Prior to the Select Board considering the request, chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti invited Patel or his representative to explain the proposed language and any pertinent details.

On behalf of Patel, his attorney Arthur M. Pearlman spoke before the board to request that the town file the petition with the state Legislature as soon as possible. He added that the language used in the current draft is the same language that had been approved by the Legislature for another client of his.

Ferretti then turned to the rest of the board members for their perspectives.

“I may not have agreed when this was going before Town Meeting, however it passed pretty overwhelmingly at Town Meeting,” Select Board member Peter J. Meier said. “I think we need to respect the will of the people who voted that evening and forward this along to the state Legislature for action.”

Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian and member Jeanne L. Azarovitz agreed with Meier’s statement.

Select Board clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo stated she had concerns about the current draft’s language and preferred to submit the same language for H&J Convenience at 365 Barlows Landing Road, as the board had just approved for Cataumet Fish.

Ferretti asked Bertram about his opinion on the suggestion, to which Bertram replied that “it’s really more of a policy question.”

In response to Mastrangelo, Pearlman stated that the language of the current draft reflects the vote at Town Meeting. “I think the people before us specifically requested the language they got,” he said. “When we went before the Town Meeting, we specifically did not include that language.”

Pearlman emphasized that the draft’s language restricts the license to the location, as specifically asked for at Town Meeting.

“If we stop using [the license], it goes back to the town. You have now a special license for you. It doesn’t disappear. It’s there,” Pearlman stated. “And if someone comes along at some point in time and wants to operate a business at 365 [Barlows Landing Road], you can have them go to a hearing and if you approve them, they can have the license.”

Mastrangelo stated that the board had just approved language for the special legislation for Cataumet Fish at 1360 Route 28A, which stated that “if the license was canceled, revoked or no longer in use, that it would be reissued at the same location within three years.”

She recommended that this same language be used in the special legislation for Patel at 365 Barlows Landing Road.

Siroonian said she “did not disagree with that for consistency purposes.”

The board directed Bertram to amend the draft to add that language to make it consistent with the special legislation liquor licenses and provide a copy of the updated version to Pearlman prior to the board’s next meeting on Tuesday, June 30.
