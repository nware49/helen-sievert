All 32 Articles Pass At Bourne Town Meeting

By Helen Sievert

May 6, 2026

All articles on both the Annual and Special Town Meeting warrants were approved by the approximately 289 Bourne residents in attendance on Monday night , May 4 , with the annual warrant featuring 29 articles and the special warrant totaling three.

Held in the Bourne High School auditorium, the Town Meeting votes were cast primarily by voice votes and standing votes for certain articles after voters experienced issues with their electronic clickers.

Annual Town Meeting

Annual Town Meeting warrant articles determined spending for Fiscal Year 2027.

Voters approved Articles 1 through 5, which were designated as essential articles because their approval was required for the town government to function legally and fund services.

These articles featured the consent agenda in Article 1 , the town’s FY27 operating budget of $84,586,115 in Article 2 , FY27 Sewer Enterprise Fund of $1,949,450 in Article 3 , FY27 Integrated Solid Waste Management Program enterprise operating budget of $15,380,924 in Article 4 and $2,961,350 for capital outlay projects requested by the Engineering Department, Shore and Harbor Department, Police and Fire departments, Bourne Public Schools, Department of Public Works, Sewer Department and ISWM in Article 5 .

Voters also approved Article 6 , which rescinded the vote taken during the May 2024 Annual Town Meeting that had authorized the town to borrow funds for school wastewater treatment plant repairs. Because the scope of work on the plant changed and different funds are being used, the previously approved borrowing is no longer needed.

Voters approved two articles related to the relocation of three Algonquin Gas Transmission pipelines, with the Massachusetts Department of Transportation having asked Enbridge, the pipelines’ owner, to move them due to upcoming construction for the bridges replacement project.

Enbridge sought the approval to use 2.76 acres of town-owned, Conservation Commission-managed land, which includes portions of the Sacrifice Rock Woods and Nightingale Pond conservation area, as land for the project. State law requires that Enbridge provide replacement land for the parcels.

Article 7 authorized the Select Board and Conservation Commission to transfer the parcels to Enbridge. Article 8 authorized the Select Board to petition the state Legislature to file legislation that would allow the Town of Bourne and its Conservation Commission to use the land.

After a brief inquiry into the work involved in the project, voters approved Article 9 , authorizing the betterment and acceptance of Spinnaker Lane and Daniel Webster Lane as public ways; they are currently private. The town had received a petition from the homeowners on the roads.

The article authorized the town to borrow $602,250 for the construction and repair of the roads to meet the town’s regulations, allowing it to then be accepted by the town. That cost would be paid by a tax betterment assessed to the homeowners on the roads.

Voters, both proponents and an opponent, shared their opinions on Article 10 , ultimately approving the request for $1,038,373.70 for the resurfacing and temporary repairs of Tara Terrace, Eldridge Street and Eldridge Street Extension. At various public meetings, residents and board and committee members alike described deep and wide potholes and sunken divots in the roads.

Unlike the previous article, the roads will remain private and not become public. The town had also received a petition from homeowners on the roads.

William Chapman of Tara Terrace said a betterment should be done for each street individually, rather than combining the streets into a single betterment project. Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram and Town Engineer Timothy Lydon both said the town’s bylaw and Massachusetts General Law allow multiple roads to be included in a single betterment project.

Two Tara Terrace residents spoke in favor of the betterment. Ashley Schulz said the dangerous road conditions resulted in her daughter breaking her arm and in several car repairs. Janet Butler noted the various means and efforts that the neighbors in favor of betterment have taken over the course of the past 10 years.

“As neighbors, we can’t collect a million dollars by passing a can to make this happen,” Butler said, referencing the betterment cost. “We’ve done that to keep the roads going for the past 10 years.”

“This doesn’t come out of taxpayer dollars,” she added. “All of this will be put on our tax bills; we’re willing to pay it, and we’re asking you to support us to put it back together.” The sum of this betterment will be repaid through betterments assessed to the homeowners on the roads.

“I’m not opposed to having this done, but the roads—I don’t know that there’s any legal balance for me to be responsible for paying for frontage on roads that I have nothing at all to do with,” Chapman said.

No reports were shared in relation to Article 11 , which allows committees and town and state officers to share reports, recommendations and updates with Town Meeting.

Articles 12 through 20 are funded through the Community Preservation Act (CPA) and sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee (CPC). The funds can be spent on several types of projects, including affordable housing, historic preservation, open space and recreation.

Voters approved Articles 12, 14, 15, 18, 19 and 20 with no discussion.

Those items included the Recreation Department’s request for $400,000 for the design and construction of the Cataumet Schoolhouse hardcourt project and Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) Improvement Project; the Facilities Department’s request for $77,0000 to fund the design costs for the repair of the exterior of Town Hall and the School Department’s request for $30,000 for a feasibility study for repairs of the central school administration building at 36 Sandwich Road; Bourne Affordable Housing Trust’s request for $111,500 to support its FY27 budget and affordable housing services; CPC’s request for $75,000 for its administrative and operating expenses for FY27; and the appropriation of $1,931,502.05 from FY27 estimated CPA revenues for the Community Preservation Fund Special Purpose Reserves.

Articles 13, 16 and 17 were approved by voters after longer discussions.

The Recreation Department’s request for $350,000 in Article 13 for the Bourne Veterans Memorial Community Center shade structures project was approved. The project will include two 15-foot cantilevered shade structures and ADA-compliant seating and walkways.

Resident William Doherty inquired about the maintenance required for the structures, seating and walkways, to which Recreation Committee clerk Karen Wilson pointed to the committee’s proactive approach of trying to “replace areas that needed a lot of maintenance with things that don’t need as much to last longer.”

Recreation Committee member James Linsky asked the Finance Committee to explain why it voted 4-1 against recommending the project. “Whichever way you go, you’re going to have an expense,” Finance Committee vice chairwoman Amanda Bongiovanni said of maintaining either cantilevered shade structures or a natural approach, such as trees. The committee preferred trees because “in the end, it might be more cost-effective than a structure.”

Article 16 authorizes the Select Board to purchase a conservation restriction for $325,000 for 940 County Road, a vacant 7.35-acre wooded lot in Pocasset, as well as $10,000 for “incidental” costs.

Bourne Conservation Trust (BCT), a private nonprofit group, recently bought the land for $650,000 for conservation purposes and proposed a partnership with the Town of Bourne under which the CPA funds would be used to purchase a conservation restriction on the property, preserving it in perpetuity for the public. Per the CPA, open-space parcels must be owned by the town for conservation or recreational purposes, or by a nonprofit.

Having been the sole dissenter in the Select Board’s vote to recommend the article, board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian explained her vote, emphasizing the need for a “healthy balance” between preserving open space and providing affordable single-family homes.

Conversely, Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo supported the article, specifically the partnership between the BCT and the town. “They can go in and make the deal, and then we can help them, and support by sharing the cost for the conservation restriction and guarantee that it is open to the public,” she said.

Article 17 , the Housing Assistance Corporation’s (HAC) request for $100,000 for the renovation of 460 West Main Street in Hyannis to house the Assistance Resource Shelter, Cape Cod’s only state-licensed emergency overnight individual shelter, was approved by voters after the night’s longest discussion. HAC is seeking $100,000 from every Cape town.

Many residents spoke in favor of the article, sharing anecdotes and information about the need for the emergency shelter, including Robert Zibbell, Bourne Housing Partnership chairwoman Susan Ross and Stéphane Ruauct, who also works as an outreach specialist at HAC and was the former director of St. Joseph’s House Shelter.

William Doherty inquired if individuals from Bourne would be given preference at the shelter.

“We need to do this,” Ross said, noting that the Bourne Housing Partnership and Trust voted unanimously to support the article. “We are the second-highest town of the Cape to use these facilities—Barnstable, and then Bourne. They are not going to say no to somebody because they are not from Bourne…It’s Cape Cod as a community.”

Gene Curry, a Bourne resident and attorney for HAC, reiterated Ross’s point that a significant number of Bourne residents have participated in the various programs.

Residents Christine Powell and Julie Hull asked the Finance Committee to explain its 2-2-1 vote recommending the article, since the Select Board had voted unanimously to support it.

Prefacing that the committee is in favor of emergency housing shelter, Finance Committee member Arthur Wayne Sampson explained that he voted against the article due to a lack of information and clarity.

Resident Steven Lucas asked Sampson, “Is there one reason in the world that anybody in this country should be homeless?” Sampson replied, “It’s not a question of whether or not they should be homeless. Our job, our responsibility for the residents of this town, is to get clear information so that we can make a clear decision.”

Finance Committee clerk Carla Emmons said she was in favor of the article as she has observed the need for such resources, like the emergency shelter, as a member of the St. Vincent Committee. “When you do meet somebody in that situation, it’s very humbling, and you wonder how they can continue in this process of not having a lot of resources,” she said, noting the need for the programs and services provided by the emergency shelter.

Resident Ronald Everitt agreed with Lucas that “nobody should be homeless” but stated the need for additional clarity about the article.

CPC chairman Barry H. Johnson, who is also town clerk, noted that prior to CPC’s recommendation of the article, the committee frequently engaged in correspondence and had a “great deal of discussion” with HAC as well as Associate Town Counsel Rian R. Holmquest. “Every single condition that we asked to have on this article, Housing Assistance Corporation has agreed to,” Johnson said.

Resident Steven Strojny made a motion to vote on the article. A standing vote was conducted, with 199 in favor and 20 opposed.

Voters approved Article 21 , which requested $160,000 of free cash be moved into the stabilization fund, as well as Article 22 , which requested that $850,000 in free cash be transferred to the capital stabilization fund.

Article 23 was approved by the voters, fulfilling the Human Services Committee’s request for a transfer of $40,000 from free cash to fund the human services grant program for FY27, which funds various human service agencies in town.

Voters approved various municipal fees. Article 24 increased the demand fees assessed on unpaid taxes on bills from $15 to $30. Article 25 increased dog-licensing fees from $7 to $10 for spayed female and neutered male dogs and from $10 to $15 for both male and female dogs. Article 26 increased birth, death and marriage certificate fees from $10 to $15, and marriage intentions fees from $25 to $40.

Article 27 , which requested that $115,962 be raised and appropriated to the Special Education Reserve Fund, was approved.

Voters also approved two amendments to the town’s zoning bylaws.

Article 28 amended the Town of Bourne’s accessory dwelling unit (ADU) zoning bylaw to align it with state regulations. The proposed changes include allowing ADUs in downtown neighborhood districts, changing the required number of parking spaces for ADUs and removing lot size and owner-occupancy requirements.

Article 29 was also approved, which additionally amended the Town of Bourne’s zoning bylaw to increase the maximum front setback from 15 to 30 feet for parcels that are located in the downtown district but not on a primary street.

Special Town Meeting was called primarily to allow a vote on a budget adjustment for FY26. A citizen’s petition was also included on the warrant.

Votes reached the 9/10 supermajority required for Article 1 , approving the request for $13,334.18 to pay unpaid bills from Fiscal Year 2025, including a transfer of $11,675.38 from free cash to pay a missed encumbrance of one month’s legal bills, and $1,658.80 from ISWM retained earnings to pay for unpaid ISWM bills.

Requiring a simple majority vote, Article 2 was also approved. It appropriated $470,000 in free cash to Public Works to assist in covering the $800,000 deficit in the FY26 snow and ice budget as a result of this past winter’s storms.

Article 3 , a citizen’s petition by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H&J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms, was approved by voters after reaching the simple majority required. The vote authorizes the Select Board to petition the state Legislature for special legislation for an annual all-alcoholic beverages retail license for an off-premises site for 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset.

Neither the Finance Committee nor the Select Board made a positive motion on the article because, as Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram said previously, the license Patel is applying for is not available because it has already been granted. He added that the article can be included at Special Town Meeting, but since the Select Board is the licensing authority, Town Meeting cannot grant a liquor license.

A positive motion came from the floor from resident Joan Eccleston, who also shared a positive character statement for Patel.

A standing vote was conducted, with 193 in favor and 49 opposed.

The applicant will schedule a time for discussion with the Select Board at a later date.
