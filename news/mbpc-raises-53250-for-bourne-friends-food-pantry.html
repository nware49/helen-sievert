MBPC Raises $53,250 For Bourne Friends Food Pantry

By Helen Sievert

February 18, 2026

After raising $53,250 through its annual polar plunge and potluck dinner and dance, which featured auctions and raffles, the Monument Beach Polar Club (MBPC) donated the funds to the Bourne Friends Food Pantry on February 12.

The club has been donating to the food pantry for the past 26 years. This year’s goal was to meet or beat last year’s total of $62,000. To date, MBPC has donated a total of $392,820 to the pantry.

A snow squall occurred just before the plunge on January 1, prompting many to call MBPC president Robert W. Bilodeau to ask if it had been canceled. The event is on, he told callers, “It’s a polar plunge!”

Nevertheless, he said, the club raised $53,250 and “did exceptionally [well] for having half the people,” compared to last year, at the plunge, and at the potluck dinner and dance.

The inclement weather on the day of the plunge and the dinner impacted turnouts for both events. On average 225 to 275 participants usually plunge. This year the official head count revealed that 125 people participated.

Of the total donation, approximately $3,500 worth of registrations, donations and sponsors were collected at the beach on January 1, $6,400 was collected from pre-regsitrations, donations, sponsors through Venmo, $36,000 from donations, $300 from the 2025 summer food drive, $1,000 monies from the MBPC account to start and $6,000 from the dinner, which was composed of $4,000 from admissions, donation and auctions, $1,700 from raffles and $300 from Venmo transactions.

The cause is “near and dear, to my heart,” Bilodeau said, as he remembered growing up in a family of seven on food assistance programs. Now he is “paying it forward,” he said.

The Bourne Friends Food Pantry “runs off donations,” pantry vice president Jill Fiore said, “every bit helps.”

Food donations help stock refrigerators and shelves; monetary donations help rent refrigerators and buy food from wholesale vendors, local community gardens and food banks, such as the Boston Food Bank.

Operated by five staff members and 60 volunteers who help unload, shop and stock, Bourne Friends Food Pantry president Peter Lindberg said the pantry serves the Bourne community. However, on an emergency basis it will help nonresidents and refer them to food pantries closer to their areas as well.

In addition to the donations people made to participate in the plunge itself, the club’s potluck dinner on January 31 included a live auction, can raffle, silent auction, 50/50 raffle and more than 100 prizes, all donated by local businesses.

Prizes included ice-time at Gallo Ice Arena, snowshoes, kitchenware, hair care items, a kayak, lawn equipment, tool sets, stained glass art, hand blown glass items, Girl Scout cookies, paintings, wood carvings, gift baskets, a china tea set, books, a children’s table of prizes, dozens of gift certificates to area restaurants, gift shops and more, and Titlist golf bags and assorted golf accessories. A live artist painted over the course of the evening, with the finished piece auctioned off as well.

Cape Coda, a local band, donated their time as live entertainment. Alongside a spread of homemade meals, the dinner also included donated food from Bridgeview Grill, Mezza Luna, Avocados Always Fresh, Graziela’s and Silver Beach Pizza.

Major donors also included the Watka Charitable Trust, the Heidi Ferguson Education Trust and the Wings Neck Team.

MBPC has tentativley scheduled a food drive on Sunday, June 28, from 7 to 11 AM at the Bourne Friends Food Pantry at 121 Main Street in Buzzards Bay, to mark the halfway point until the next annual polar plunge to celebrate 2027. Donations of non-perishables or hygiene items will be accepted. For more information, visit MBPC’s Facebook page, @monumentbeachpolarclub .

For more information about Bourne Friends Food Pantry, visit friendsofbournecoa.org/friends-food-pantry/ .
