Bourne Town Meeting Passes All 32 Articles

By Helen Sievert

May 5, 2026

Approximately 289 Bourne residents voted to pass all the articles on the Annual and Special Town Meeting warrants on Monday night, May 4, with the annual warrant featuring 29 articles and the special warrant totaling three articles.

Both sessions were held in the Bourne High School Auditorium, with the annual warrant determining the spending for the upcoming Fiscal Year 2027, and the special warrant adjusting the current FY26.

Article 1 , the consent agenda that includes regular required authorizations, elected officials’ salaries, Chapter 90, revolving funds and accrued contractual compensated absences, was approved by the voters.

Voters approved Article 2 , which requested an appropriation of $84,586,115 for the proposed Town of Bourne’s Fiscal Year 2027 operating budget. The budget represents a 2.63% increase over FY26, includes debt service costs, and reflects the proposed adjustment to the FY26 snow and ice budget. The budget, constrained by service demands and shared costs, is now balanced following discussions and adjustments over the past couple of months.

Article 3 was approved by the voters, which requested $1,949,450 for the FY27 Sewer Enterprise Fund. It also requested that “$181,283 be transferred to the General Fund to offset Sewer Enterprise indirect expenses" and "move that the sum of $2,030,733 be raised from Sewer Enterprise receipts and $100,000 be transferred from Sewer Enterprise retained earnings.”

Voters approved Article 4 . It had requested $15,380,924 for the FY27 Integrated Solid Waste Management Program (ISWM) Enterprise operating budget. The warrant also requests that “$2,784,942 be transferred to the General Fund to offset ISWM Enterprise indirect expenses, and in order to meet this appropriation, the sum of $18,165,866 is being proposed to be raised from ISWM Enterprise receipts.”

Article 5 was approved by voters. It featured a number of requests totaling $2,961,350 to cover the costs of capital outlay projects proposed by the Engineering Department, Shore and Harbor Department, Police and Fire departments, Bourne Public Schools, Department of Public Works, Sewer Department and ISWM. Funding sources include free cash, Waterways Improvement Fund, enterprise retained earnings and enterprise borrowing.

Voters approved Article 6 , which requested that Town Meeting “rescind the vote taken under Article 5-12 of the May 2024 Annual Town Meeting,” which had authorized the Town to borrow funds for school wastewater treatment plant repairs. Because the scope of work on the wastewater treatment plant changed and different funds are being used, the previously approved borrowing is no longer needed.

Voters approved two articles related to the relocation of three Algonquin Gas Transmission pipelines, with the Massachusetts Department of Transportation having asked Enbridge, the pipelines’ owners, to move the pipelines due to upcoming construction for the bridge replacement project.

Enbridge sought approval to use 2.76 acres of town-owned, Conservation Commission-managed land, which includes portions of the Sacrifice Rock Woods and Nightingale Pond conservation area, as land for the project. State law required that Enbridge provide replacement land for the aforementioned parcels.

Through Article 7 , Enbridge requested that the Select Board be authorized to petition the state Legislature to file legislation that would allow the Town of Bourne and its Conservation Commission to use the land.

Article 8 authorized the Select Board and Conservation Commission to transfer the parcels to Enbridge.

After a brief inquiry about the work involved in the project, voters approved Article 9 , authorizing the betterment and acceptance of Spinnaker Lane and Daniel Webster Lane as public ways; they are currently private ways. The town had received a petition from the homeowners on the roads.

The article authorized the town to borrow $602,250 for the construction and repair of the roads to meet the town’s regulations. This cost would be paid by tax betterments assessed to the homeowners on the road.

Voters, both proponents and an opponent, shared their opinions on Article 10 , ultimately approving the request for $1,038,373.70 for the resurfacing and temporary repairs of Tara Terrace, Eldridge Street and Eldridge Street Extension. Unlike the previous article, the roads will remain private and not become public roads. The town had also received a petition from homeowners on the roads. The sum will be repaid through betterments assessed to the homeowners on the roads.

No reports were shared in relation to Article 11 , which allows committees and town or state officers to share reports, recommendations and updates with Town Meeting.

Articles 12 through 20 are funded through the Community Preservation Act and sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee. The funds can be spent on several types of projects, including affordable housing, historic preservation and open space/recreation.

Voters approved the Recreation Department’s request for $400,000 in Article 12 in relation to the design and construction of the Cataumet Schoolhouse hardcourt project, which would feature a new post-tension concrete court with a 20-year guarantee and fencing to address disrepair, as well as improvements to make the parking, path and court compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

The Recreation Department’s request for $350,000 in Article 13 for the Bourne Veterans’ Memorial Community Center shade structures project was also approved by voters after residents inquired about maintenance, and the Finance Committee was asked to expand on its 4-1 recommendation vote against the project. The project will include two 15-foot cantilevered shade structures and ADA-compliant seating and walkways.

Voters approved the Facilities Department request for $77,0000 in Article 14 to “fund the design costs associated with repairing the exterior envelope of Town Hall, including, but not limited to, design of masonry and window repairs to prevent water infiltration.”

Approved by voters, Article 15 had been the School Department's request for $30,000 for a feasibility study of the central school administration building at 36 Sandwich Road to address a number of repairs.

Article 16 was approved by voters after Select Board members stated two different opinions, which authorized the Select Board to purchase a conservation restriction for $325,000 for 940 County Road, a vacant 7.35 acre-wooded lot in Pocasset, as well as $10,000 for “incidental” costs.

Bourne Conservation Trust (BCT), a private nonprofit group, had recently bought the land for $650,000 for conservation purposes and proposed a partnership with the Town of Bourne, under which the CPA funds would be used to purchase a conservation restriction on the property, preserving it in perpetuity for the public. Per the CPA, open-space parcels must be owned by the town for conservation or recreational purposes or be owned by a nonprofit.

The Housing Assistance Corporation’s (HAC) request for $100,000 for the renovation of 460 West Main Street in Hyannis to house the Assistance Resource Shelter, Cape Cod’s only state-licensed emergency overnight individual shelter, was approved by the voters after the night’s longest discussion.

Proponents of Article 17 cited the need and importance of the shelter, while opponents expressed the need for additional information and clarity.

The Bourne Affordable Housing Trust’s request for $111,500 in Article 18 to support its FY27 budget and affordable housing services was approved by voters. The partnership is solely financed through CPA funds.

Voters approved the Community Preservation Committee's request for $75,000 as noted in Article 19 for the CPC’s administrative and operating expenses for Fiscal Year 2027. The funds would cover administrative services and costs, with the remainder going back to the Community Preservation Funds.

Article 20 was approved by voters, appropriating $1,931,502.05 from FY27 estimated CPA revenues for the Community Preservation Fund Special Purpose Reserves. The article recommended that $1,502,279.37 would be for the reserve for open space; $214,611.34 would be for the reserve for community housing; and $214,611.34 for the reserve for historic preservation.

Voters approved Article 21, which had requested that $160,000 of free cash be moved into the stabilization fund. Article 22, which requested that $850,000 in free cash be transferred to the capital stabilization fund, was also approved.

Article 23 was approved by the voters. The Human Services Committee requested a $40,000 transfer from free cash to fund the human services grant program for FY27, which supports various human service agencies in town.

Voters approved Article 24 , increasing the demand fees assessed on unpaid taxes on bills, such as mooring and marina, sewer charges, boat excise, motor vehicle excise, and property taxes, from $15 to $30.

Article 25 was approved as well, resulting in an increase in dog licensing fees from $7 to $10 for spayed female and neutered male dogs, and from $10 to $15 for both male and female dogs.

Voters approved Article 26, which increased birth, death, and marriage certificate fees from $10 to $15, and marriage intentions fees from $25 to $40.

Article 27 , which requested that $115,962 be raised and appropriated to the Special Education Reserve Fund, was approved.

Voters approved Article 28 , amending the Town of Bourne’s accessory dwelling unit (ADU) zoning bylaw to align it with state regulations. The proposed changes include allowing ADUs in downtown neighborhood districts, changing the required number of parking spaces for ADUs and removing lot size and owner-occupancy requirements.

Article 29 was also approved, which additionally amended the Town of Bourne’s zoning bylaw to increase the maximum front setback from 15 feet to 30 feet for parcels that are located in the downtown district, but not on a primary street.

The Special Town Meeting was primarily called to allow a vote on a budget adjustment for FY26. A citizen’s petition was also included on the special warrant.

Article 1 had requested $13,334.18 to pay unpaid bills from the Fiscal Year 2025, including a transfer of $11,675.38 from free cash to pay a missed encumbrance of one month’s legal bills, and $1,658.80 from ISWM retained earnings to pay for unpaid ISWM bills. Voters approved the article.

Approved by the voters, Article 2 appropriated $470,000 in free cash to Public Works. This helps cover the $800,000 deficit in the FY26 snow and ice budget.

Article 3, a citizen’s petition by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H & J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms, was approved by the voters. The vote authorizes the Select Board to petition the Legislature for a special legislature for an annual all alcoholic beverages license for an off-premise license for 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset.
