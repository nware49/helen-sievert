Bourne Sewer Commission OKs MMA Sewer Account Closure, $3,948 Fee Abatement

By Helen Sievert

March 27, 2026

The Bourne Board of Sewer Commissioners approved Massachusetts Maritime Academy’s (MMA) request for an abatement and the closure of the sewer account at 16 Tower Lane during its meeting on Tuesday , March 24 , subject to two conditions.

In addition to permanently disconnecting from the sewer system, MMA, on behalf of the MMA Foundation, which is the property owner, requested an abatement of $3,949.48 for sewer fees for the second half of Fiscal Year 2026.

After reviewing the request, Department of Public Works Director Matthew R. Sawicki recommended an abatement of $3,948, and he said MMA must still pay the remaining $1.48, which represents the interest and demand fees accrued as of March 20, with the original payment having been due on March 13. He said such interest and demand fees cannot be waived per policy.

A building previously located on the property was removed to make way for the installation of a prefabricated garage, which will be used to store vehicles, grounds and facilities equipment, and support shipping and receiving activities.

The garage “does not have toilet or washroom facilities and therefore will not reconnect to the sewer system or need a septic system,” Kathy Driscoll of MMA stated in a letter to the Board of Sewer Commissioners on March 11.

Having disconnected from the town’s sewer and requested its permanent removal, MMA submitted plans for a tight tank to collect floor drain runoff.

“Probably about eight or nine months ago, I was contacted by their engineer, who explained to me that the only drain they would have would be a floor drain and they would be washing equipment inside the garage and such. So that was definitely something we did not want going into the sewer system,” Sawicki said, noting the garage would not feature any bathroom or sanitary facilities, so “we did recommend a tight tank.”

Sawicki said the Board of Health was notified to confirm whether the setup would be acceptable. “They’re familiar with what we’ve done with the process with this project,” he said.

Board of Sewer Commissioners clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo asked whether the Board of Health had already approved the tight tank or if approval would be required in the future. “We’re very limited in what we are allowed to approve with Mass state building codes with Mass Maritime,” Sawicki replied. “They did not say they would approve it or not. I haven’t heard from them,” adding that he could contact Health Director Kaitlyn Shea regarding the matter.

Inquiring whose jurisdiction the tight tank’s approval would fall under, Mastrangelo suggested that the approval of MMA’s requests be “subject to the approval of the tight tank from the Board of Health, if required.” She added, “If there’s not a tight tank, then it may have to come back to us for further discussion.”

“All measures of the construction will need to pass through the state building inspector and sign off,” Driscoll said, adding that the tight tank “is one of the items that he will have to approve for use of the property.”

Board of Sewer Commissioners member Peter J. Meier asked whether a sprinkler system would be installed. Driscoll confirmed that it would. “We do have water service coming into the building. It’s just that we won’t have any sewer services coming out,” she said. “It’ll be secondary wash waters that will be collected in a subsurface tank that will be pumped out.”

Sawicki sought to confirm that MMA understood that if a sewer connection is needed on the property in the future, a new application for the connection would be required. Driscoll said she understood.

The Board of Sewer Commissioners voted 4-0, with vice chairwoman Jeanne L. Azarovitz absent, to approve the abatement and closure of the sewer account at 16 Tower Lane, subject to the payment of interest and penalties due and the approval of the tight tank by the Board of Health, if required, or the state building inspector.
