Bourne Historical Commission Approves Three Remodeling Requests

By Helen Sievert

July 17, 2026

The Bourne Historical Commission approved requests for construction on three properties in Bourne, all older than 75 years, at its demolition delay hearing on Tuesday , July 14 .

Town records show that 28 Wenaumet Bluffs Drive in Pocasset was built in 1940 and is 86 years old; 46 Siasconset Drive in Sagamore Beach was built in 1935 and is 91 years old; and 350 Circuit Avenue in Pocasset was built in 1910 and is 116 years old.

Section 3.14.1 of the town bylaws defines a significant building as “any building or portion thereof which in whole or in part is more than seventy-five (75) years old or is listed in the historical survey of the Town of Bourne and on file with the Bourne Historical Commission.”

The bylaw further states that it is in effect to “preserve and protect historically significant buildings within the Town of Bourne that reflect distinctive features of the architectural, cultural, economic, political or social history of the Town.”

The property at 28 Wenaumet Bluffs Drive has “no historical significance,” Thomas Donovan of Donovan Construction Inc., in Pocasset said as he was requesting on behalf of property owners Scott R. and Melissa C. Murphy to rebuild the existing garage located on the property that would also feature an apartment.

“The main thing is the structure itself is in disrepair; it needs to be redone,” Donovan added, citing the large amounts of “rot along most of the lower areas of the building” and the condition of the rafters as Kelly Donovan-Hannah of Donovan Construction showed the commission photos of the garage.

“The rafters, when they reroofed the building...it looks like probably eight to 10 years ago, instead of fixing the roofline and putting rafters up correctly, they just scabbed on two-by-sixes to existing rafters to try to get the bow out of it,” he added.

Donovan also said the project would require elevating the garage. He explained that a new building code was instituted in Massachusetts that stated that when a building is located in an “AE-15 floodplain, the first floor level now has to be an elevation 17.”

A Federal Emergency Management Agency-designated special flood hazard area, zone AE, indicates that the location is a high-flood-risk area, as it has a 1% annual chance of flooding. The “15” reflects that the location has a base-flood elevation of 15 feet above sea level, meaning that floods are estimated to rise 15 feet.

Since the septic system was redone “approximately a year and a half ago,” allowing a one-bedroom apartment in the garage, Donovan said that it will “meet some of the goals of the affordable housing that we have as a criteria in the town with having [accessory dwelling units].”

Historical Commission chairman Neil F. Langille sought to confirm that the new garage would maintain the current garage’s footprint, to which Donovan said that it would. “We’re keeping the existing foundation, the existing slab.”

He added that the new garage would “match the house” in keeping with the “same Cape architecture as the neighborhood.”

A motion by commission member Judith A. Riordan to approve the application as presented was seconded by member Jack MacDonald. The commission then unanimously voted in the affirmative.

Zachary Perocchi of Perocchi Building & Remodeling, based in Plymouth, made a presentation on behalf of James S.E. and Sharon A. Fluet, the owners of 46 Siasconset Drive.

“I believe the house was moved to this location,” Perocchi said. “I’m not sure at what point, but it wasn’t originally built here.”

The proposal was to build a six-by-eight composite deck and eyebrow roof covering the deck’s footprint, which would be supported by two columns to create an entryway to “stay out of the rain,” Perocchi said. It would also feature composite decking and composite trim.

Additional work would also be done to ensure that the new eyebrow roof would be flush with the existing roof.

In response to Riordan’s inquiry if “essentially it is going to look the same,” Perocchi replied, “Yes, essentially it is going to look the same. The only thing that is going to change is this eyebrow that is going to be added.”

Commission member Donald J. Mahoney asked about the material in the columns and the schematics of the deck’s steps, to which Perocchi replied that the roof would be supported by one-by-eight-foot composite square columns and that there would be one step for going onto the deck and one step going into the house.

Mahoney followed up by asking how far the eyebrow roof would extend out in front of the house. Perocchi said the roof would extend out to cover the deck, which would be six-by-eight feet.

Perocchi also said that the original plan was to have the deck extend five feet out from the house; however, after Building Commissioner Eleanor MacKay informed him that it would require footings “to be the depth of the foundation, which would be about 10 feet of footing,” she recommended that if he “came out just six feet, we could just do typical 12-inch footings.”

“That’s why we’re proposing to come out six feet,” Perocchi said. “So I don’t have to put a 10-foot footing in to support this small roof.”

The eyebrow roof over the front deck would provide shelter from the rain as people enter the house, Susan Fluet said as her husband showed the commission current photos of the house.

As the owner of 350 Circuit Avenue in Pocasset, Nancy Carr presented a proposal to incorporate a portion of the house’s covered wraparound porch to expand the home’s interior space.

Carr said that she and her family have loved using the house for approximately 30 years as a three-season home. However, since the family has grown to include her three children and nine grandchildren, she contracted Craig R. Ellis Builders of Pocasset to create additional living space to accommodate the growing family.

“What I’d like to do is just use the wraparound porch—the side of the porch—for living space,” Carr said. “So what we’ve come up with is just literally push that side of the house out,” separating the front and the back porch.

“You’re enclosing a porch,” Riordan said. “The exterior will be the same? The materials?”

“Exactly,” Carr confirmed.

In response to Mahoney’s inquiry about the use of shingles, Carr said the exterior would be shingled and “look exactly the same.”
