Community Preservation Committee Recommends Requests

By Helen Sievert

March 6, 2026

Various requests for Community Preservation Act (CPA) funds for Fiscal Year 2027 were presented for comment at the Bourne Community Preservation Committee (CPC) meeting on Wednesday , March 4 , ranging from building maintenance to recreational initiatives.

All the requests will be funded from existing funds or reserve funds. These requests will also be presented at a joint meeting between the Bourne Select Board and Finance Committee on Monday, March 16.

School Department

CPC and Open Space Committee chairman Barry H. Johnson presented on behalf of the Bourne School Department Director of Business Services Jordan Geist, who was unable to attend the meeting due to a Bourne School Committee meeting at the same time. Geist had requested $30,000 for a feasibility study of the central school administration building at 36 Sandwich Road.

The study would assess the siding and trim, which are both nearing the end of their useful lives; the front ramp and stairs, which are cracked; the rusted back entrance staircase; and water penetration at the back entrance.

Neil F. Langille, the Historical Commission’s representative on the CPC, shared that the commission had unanimously voted on February 10 to move the project forward after Geist presented it.

CPC voted unanimously to approve the application and recommend it for Town Meeting.

Town Hall Facilities

Facilities Director Steven Gavazza requested $77,000 for an evaluation of Bourne Town Hall, which would include design services and a pre-bid support package to address water damage and window, roof and facade repairs. It would also feature hazardous material testing, design development, contract procurement and field investigation, Gavazza said.

The request was also unanimously approved for Town Meeting by the CPC.

Conservation Restriction

President Jack Dawley of the Bourne Conservation Trust, BCT trustee Steve Ballentine and Executive Director Mark Robinson of The Compact of Cape Cod Conservation Trusts, Inc., requested that the town purchase a conservation restriction for 940 County Road, a vacant wooded lot in Pocasset.

As a developable parcel, BCT sought to acquire the 7.34-acre lot for conservation purposes and ultimately purchased the land from its previous owner for $650,000. The trust’s interest in the property stemmed from its proximity to other current or future conservation areas.

The trust proposed a partnership with the Town of Bourne, under which CPA funds would be used to purchase a conservation restriction for the property. Totaling to $325,000, half of the seller’s appraised value, the restriction would preserve the property in perpetuity.

By way of nearby cranberry bogs and roads, the Redbrook Pond Conservation Area can be reached on foot from the property. Additionally, since the Town of Bourne has a conservation restriction on the abutting Barnstable County Hospital property, the title for the property would eventually belong to the town after an act of the Legislature.

In addition to providing a passive recreation market trail, it would also provide wetland protection to a 3.5-acre maple swamp located on the county hospital property and add to the existing habitat protection area. By conserving abutting vacant land, the trust can create larger areas of refuge for animals.

Rather than have the town buy the land, the trust is requesting that the town buy a conservation restriction for the land. This approach continues the partnership between the town and the trust to conserve land. The trust initially negotiates, purchases the land, and privately raises funds for the project, after which the town obtains a conservation restriction via the Community Preservation Funds, which guarantees that the land is conserved in perpetuity, as well as the public access that the CPA requires.

Per the Community Preservation Act, open space parcels must be owned by the town for conservation or recreational purposes or be owned by a nonprofit for which the town has a conservation restriction.

The vision for the property is to establish a trailhead, beginning at a parking lot accessed from County Road. Additional parking would be provided by Cape Cod Christadelphians Church at 930 County Road.

Volunteers with the trust, known as “Trail Meisters,” will assist in creating a loop trail along the property’s boundaries that will allow connections to the cranberry bogs to the south and Valley Farm Road to the north, which acts as a conduit to the Red Brook Pond Conservation Area. The trail and the property would continue to be stewarded by members of the trust.

On February 18, some CPC members had questions about the proposal. Kathy Fox Alfano, the Housing Authority’s representative on the CPC, asked on February 18 if residents of the neighborhood associated with Valley Farm Road would be comfortable with the proposed plan.

“They’re ecstatic,” said Ballentine.

Laurianne Gilbert, the Recreation Committee’s representative on the CPC, inquired whether the Housing Authority had been made aware of the proposal, to which CPC chairman Barry H. Johnson replied that he would do so.

The conservation restriction must be appraised to “quantify the value of development rights of the property and enable the town to justify the cost,” said Robinson. Johnson had received an estimate of services of $2,500 and a preliminary appraisal for the property from Joe Clancy of Clancy Appraisal Co. in Falmouth. The committee unanimously voted on February 18 to approve the proposal for the appraisal. Clancy’s appraisal stated that the value of the parcel without the conservation restriction being placed on it is $700,000, and the value with the conservation restriction is $110,000, and the value of development rights is $590,000. Johnson said that the CPC will receive “his formal report in the upcoming weeks.”

On Wednesday, March 4, Gilbert inquired about how often BCT monitors the land, citing an increase in the public’s use of conservation areas. Dawley said that Trail Meisters inspect the land they are responsible for “every couple of weeks.” Using a wide communication network, they share notices of dangerous conditions or inappropriate behavior “fairly quickly” and “address them fairly promptly” thereafter.

Gilbert stated a personal concern that “if we keep purchasing this land for conservation trust, we’re never going to have these single-family homes.”

Susan Ross, a member of the Bourne Affordable Housing Trust and at-large member of the Housing Partnership, asked whether BCT had ever considered working together with affordable housing to build some attainable housing for Bourne residents along the edges of certain conservation parcels, citing that this partnership has been established in other towns. “Maybe somehow, in the future, we can work together and get a lot of conservation, but a little bit of housing,” she said.

Dawley acknowledged the housing crisis on Cape Cod, but said that Ross’s suggestion was not discussed in relation to the lot at 940 County. However, “I think there are some parcels that we see along the way where some form of limited development, or concentrated clustered kind of development, could very well be appropriate,” he said. ”It’s a case-by-case consideration.”

The request was approved by the majority of the committee to move forward to Town Meeting, with Gilbert being the sole dissenting vote.

Housing Partnership

Solely financed by the CPA, Susan Ross also presented the Bourne Housing Partnership’s request for $111,500 to support its Fiscal Year 2027 budget and affordable housing services. The funds would support legal support, continued monitoring of properties to ensure their affordability, inspections, contributions to home lotteries, Cape Cod Commission assistance, and grant writing.

John J. Duggan Jr., the Planning Board’s representative on the CPC, said that the partnership does “great work” and it is “a great use of CPA funds.” The CPC unanimously approved the request to move forward toward Town Meeting.

Housing Assistance Corp

Patricia Mitrokostas, principal director of strategic funding for Housing Assistance Corp (HAC) and Robert Brennan, chief legal officer at HAC, presented a request of $100,000 on behalf of HAC for the renovation of 460 West Main Street in Hyannis, which would house the Assistance Resource Shelter, Cape Cod’s only state-licensed emergency overnight shelter, which is currently at 77 Winter Street in Hyannis.

Operated by the Catholic Charities of the Diocese of Fall River, the shelter at 77 Winter Street “has outlived its ability to currently meet the complex needs of unhoused individuals on Cape Cod and the Islands,” Mitrokostas wrote in a letter to the Bourne CPC on February 6. The new location at 460 West Main Street is triple the size of the space for services, said Brennan. 77 Main Street would be converted into more stable and permanent housing.

HAC is seeking $100,000 from every Cape Cod town’s CPC to fund the interior renovations of 460 West Main Street. Mitrokostas said as of Wednesday, HAC has presented to the CPCs of Chatham, Sandwich, Brewster, Provincetown and Truro, all of which have approved and recommended the request to their respective Town Meetings. She said that HAC will continue applying to towns on the Cape in line with their deadlines.

This funding is separate from the proposed vision for the center. However, the vision for the larger building would allow for needs such as social services, housing and substance use to be better addressed. The shelter would also provide an opportunity for people to learn how to handle their finances and nutrition. Duffy Health Center would also be on-site to provide medical services and medications.

Mitrokostas said that she has connected with Bourne Health Director Kaitlyn Shea, Bourne Health Department implementation coordinator Stephen Berdos, Bourne Police Chief Brandon M. Esip and Fire Chief David S. Pelonzi to understand the needs of Bourne in the context of homelessness, mental health and substance use. She also referenced HAC’s office in Cataumet.

Fox Alfano shared that the Bourne Affordable Housing Trust and Bourne Housing Authority, at a joint meeting earlier on Wednesday, voted to approve the request. However, she recommended that HAC provide specific statistics regarding the number of people from Bourne who utilize the current shelter in Hyannis at the next meeting. She also noted the distance between the shelter in Hyannis and Bourne.

“You make a good point,” Mitrokostas said. “The idea of the shelter is really not for people to live there…We don’t want people commuting from there…but if they need to, we do have resources to pay for transportation, whether that’s an Uber, whether that the RTA, we are equipped to do that, and that’s part of the role of the outreach team.”

She continued adding that the emergency shelter is meant to help individuals get to the “next level” and “if that means an emergency shelter stay for a week or two, that is very possible and very feasible.” HAC operates a family shelter in Dennis currently.

In 2025, HAC served more than 400 people from Bourne. Mitrokostas said that Bourne “is one of the larger service areas…only following behind Barnstable, Falmouth and Yarmouth,” but that she would obtain more specific information as per Fox Alfano’s suggestion.

Ross cited precedent from Bourne CPC and the Town Meeting approving a Cape-wide initiative, noting that last year the Town voted to contribute to housing being built in Dennis for individuals with autism.

As per Fox Alfano’s suggestion at the last CPC meeting, Johnson obtained the town counsel’s opinion on the application. Summarizing the comments made by Rian R. Holmquest of Harrington Heep, Johnson said the “funds should be subject to a grant agreement,” meaning that, if approved, the “funds would not be dispersed until the grant agreement is fully negotiated,” which is “beyond the purview of this committee.” Town counsel recommended attaching their opinion to HAC’s application for reference.

Andrew Cooney, the Open Space Committee’s representative on the CPC, asked Brennan and Mitrokostas to answer the comments raised by town counsel. Cooney reiterated Fox Alfano’s statement that to pass this article at Town Meeting, it would be beneficial to address the comments made by the town counsel to answer people’s questions.

First, he asked about recording a deed rider to require that the property only be used for “‘temporary shelter for low-income individual.’” Mitrokostas said that since the building was purchased with American Rescue Plan Act funding that is “specifically designated for a shelter,” HAC would be willing to include that restriction.

Cooney highlighted the town counsel’s comment inquiring if Barnstable, as the host town, would “hold restriction of proof of ownership and control of the property,” to which Brennan replied that the “restriction will benefit and be held by all of the funding towns.”

Fox Alfano asked if the article does not pass at Bourne Town Meeting, will people from Bourne still be able to access the shelter. Brennan said that “it’s available to whoever in the region is in need.” Cooney inquired how the shelter would identify which town someone is from. Brennan noted that those demographics are provided during the intake process and “it is not cross-checked against who’s providing funding.”

Cooney asked about designating the project as new construction rather than a rehabilitation of existing housing. Brennan said that is because there is no existing housing at the location at this time.

Cooney then asked for clarification on the five items noted in the grant agreement. The first is a non-discrimination request. The second comment suggested that the project must be “fully funded before the CPC funds” allocated by participating towns are distributed for the renovation of the project. If this is not completed within two years, then the CPC funds would expire “if funding commitment is not achieved.” Brennan said that they “fully expect to have this fully funded,” citing various sources.

The third comment was that the “host community of Barnstable is willing to fund and host a project.” Brennan said that as a religious organization, Catholic Charities operates under the Dover Act. This means that the property is exempt from zoning permits, such as variances and special permits.

Brennan also noted that Barnstable serves as a host community to various housing projects, which “comes at a significant expense,” therefore, he does not “anticipate going to Barnstable and asking them to contribute.” “So it’s not a direct CPC funding, but it’s sort of indirect funding from their taxes and resources,” Cooney said.

With the fourth comment stating that the grant agreement would expire if construction is not completed within three years, Cooney asked about the project’s timeline. Brennan said that as soon as they receive a building permit, which he says should be issued this week, construction will begin. He also noted that HAC will handle the construction management itself and aims to complete construction by the fall.

Cooney asked about the project’s incorporation of hazardous materials surveys and demolition. HAC has conducted a site plan review with the Town of Barnstable and worked closely with the town’s building commissioner, Brennan said.

The final comment suggested that as the host community, Barnstable’s CPC and Town Meeting “would agree to fund if desired.” Cooney noted that Brennan had spoken to this earlier, that it will not be featured at Barnstable’s Town Meeting since they are not being asked to pay for funding, “besides what they already provide.” “Correct,” Brennan said.

After Cooney said he was appreciative of Brennan and Mitrokostas addressing the town counsel’s comments, the board voted unanimously to approve the request to move forward.

Recreation Department

The Bourne Recreation Department had two requests for CPA funds, both of which were presented by Recreation Committee member Alice Howe.

Howe presented a request of $400,000 for the Cataumet Schoolhouse Hardcourt project, which would feature a new post-tension concrete court and fencing, as well as improvements to make the court compliant with the American Disabilities Act (ADA).

When the court was resurfaced in 2022, it was evaluated that the court would be useful for another three years before additional repairs would have to be made. Howe showed photos of the fence, which featured rotting wood and holes in the metal fence, as well as tree growth through the fence and on the court.

The new court would have a 20-year guarantee, which would minimize repairs. To make the court accessible and ADA-compliant, proposed plans include a path, two parking spots, and a wider gate to accommodate wheelchairs.

Johnson stated that he finds it “extremely important” for Recreation Director Kathryn Matthews to continue to and remain in contact with the Cataumet Schoolhouse Preservation Group, as she stated she would at the prior meeting.

Matthews stated that she has shared the project and its status with Ben Joyce, the Cataumet Schoolhouse’s groundskeeper, and Carole Courey, president of the preservation group, and will have two community input meetings in the future to gain feedback on the project.

Johnson asked if the Bourne Commission on Disabilities would have this project presented to them. Matthews said that Lori E. Cooney, who is vice chairwoman of the Recreation Committee and the Commission on Disabilities, has shared that a lack of quorum has made it difficult for the commission to meet. However, she shares updates with the commission on the Recreation Committee’s projects.

The project was unanimously approved to be recommended to Town Meeting.

The Recreation Committee was seeking $350,000 for the Bourne Community Center Shade Structures project. With the improvements to the Jason Comoletti basketball court, the new all-wheel skatepark and new playground underway, the committee requests these funds to provide two 15-foot cantilever shade structures due to the lack of shade.

Howe said that public feedback has shown a demand for providing shade with a seating area, stating that it could become an outdoor hub and “benefit every age.”

The committee unanimously approved the request to move forward and recommended it to Town Meeting.

The Bourne Historical Commission will review the application for the court project at Cataument Schoolhouse and repairs to Town Hall on Tuesday, March 10, after which Neil Langille will share the commission’s recommendations.

CPC Admin Request

Before adjourning the meeting, Johnson presented a request for $75,000 for the CPC’s administrative and operating expenses for Fiscal Year 2027. Requested annually, the funds cover the administrative services and costs, such as paying for an appraisal report.

The committee unanimously approved the request to move forward to Town Meeting.
