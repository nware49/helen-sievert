Local, State Officials Take A Ride On Potential MBTA Rail Service Line

By Helen Sievert

June 24, 2026

State and local officials taking a closer look at what it would take to restore year-round passenger rail service to Cape Cod, toured existing rail infrastructure between Buzzards Bay and Middleborough aboard an MBTA inspection train on June 22.

The trip was designed to give stakeholders a firsthand look at the rail corridor and explore the feasibility of connecting Buzzards Bay to the MBTA commuter rail network through Middleborough.

Year-round rail service between Boston and Hyannis ended in 1959. A connection between Buzzards Bay and Middleborough would restore direct MBTA access for Cape residents. The CapeFLYER currently uses the existing rail infrastructure but operates only on weekends between Memorial Day and Labor Day.

MBTA Chief Operating Officer Ryan Coholan welcomed a group aboard one of the agency’s inspection trains at the Buzzards Bay station, including Cape Cod Regional Transit Authority Administrator John Kennedy, state Senator Dylan A. Fernandes (D-Plymouth and Barnstable), state Representatives David T. Vieira (R-3rd Barnstable) and Steven G. Xiarhos (R-5th Barnstable), Bourne Select Board Clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo, and Select Board members Peter J. Meier and Jeanne L. Azarovitz.

Coholan explained that the MBTA refurbished three rail cars originally slated for scrapping and repurposed them as an inspection train.

The rear car featured a glass observation wall, allowing attendees to view the tracks and infrastructure that could support future passenger service between Buzzards Bay and Middleborough, where riders could connect to the South Coast Rail line.

During the trip, attendees examined the platforms at the Wareham and Middleborough stations. Coholan also highlighted two potential locations for a transfer stop in Middleborough. The group generally agreed that the first location would provide a more convenient connection to South Coast Rail than a second site located farther away.

“The rail line is there; it connects,” Coholan said of the railroad running from Buzzards Bay to Middleborough, which is “up to a reasonable passenger rail standard,” noting that adding signal infrastructure and positive train control will need to be discussed.

The goal of the day was to “have an honest conversation about what we have to do, what would be nice to do and what exists here today,” Coholan said, noting the trip provided an opportunity for stakeholders to put their “eyes on the infrastructure, talk about what’s involved.”

Coholan emphasized the importance of establishing and maintaining consistent conversations early.

“There are a lot of people who have to work together to make something like this successful,” he said.

Efforts to bring MBTA service to Bourne have been underway for years, a point acknowledged by many aboard the train.

Mastrangelo, Vieira and Kennedy referenced efforts dating back more than a decade, including Bourne’s votes at Special Town Meeting and town election in 2015 to become an MBTA community, a decision reaffirmed at Annual Town Meeting in 2020.

Mastrangelo said it is “great to see things come along.” Vieira added that it was “great to see where it is now.” Kennedy said it was “gratifying” to witness the “political support to get it done.”

Fernandes echoed those sentiments, noting that Bourne remains in compliance with the MBTA Communities Act, which requires municipalities served by the MBTA to establish multifamily zoning districts by right in an effort to encourage middle-income housing development.

The town maintained compliance after voters approved a multifamily housing overlay district in Buzzards Bay at the October 2025 Special Town Meeting. Communities that comply remain eligible for state grants and other forms of support.

Coholan said Monday’s trip offered a chance to “talk about what the next steps could look like, what a future connection into the existing MBTA commuter rail network could look like.”

The ride also gave attendees an opportunity to learn more about rail operations and infrastructure. Azarovitz said the experience helped illustrate both the opportunities and challenges involved. Coholan noted that constructing a passenger platform would likely be one of the easier aspects of the project, while assembling the crews and equipment needed to operate and maintain service “will take time.”

Mastrangelo said having MBTA rail service in Buzzards Bay would make the area more “attractive” and suggested a pilot program could be a logical next step. She added that “getting from an idea to reality takes a long time” and that it is “great to see something tangible.”

Azarovitz and Meier both said the trip helped them better understand what the service could look like from a rider’s perspective.

In addition to the transportation benefits, Meier said the journey demonstrated “how efficient it can be if it’s done right.”

Attendees also discussed the broader impacts of extending MBTA service to Buzzards Bay, including promoting alternative transportation, supporting business growth, accommodating housing development and addressing future parking needs.

The group brainstormed potential parking solutions, including partnerships with owners of existing parking areas in both Buzzards Bay and Middleborough.

Coholan said parking needs would be evaluated once the scope of the project becomes clearer.

Financing would most likely be a “a blend of both state, federal funding,” Coholan said, but noted that the amount would be determined after the “magnitude” of the project is finalized.

“Those dollars are so important,” he added. “We need to spend them wisely.”

The popularity of the seasonal CapeFLYER service was frequently cited as evidence of demand for expanded passenger rail service on the Cape.

“The smartest investment is leveraging existing infrastructure,” which “is precisely what this is,” Kennedy said.

Fernandes noted that expanded service to Buzzards Bay would improve rail access for both Cape Cod and the Islands, regions that currently have limited transportation options.

Several participants also emphasized that the project would do more than create a direct connection to Boston. It would expand access to the broader MBTA rail network.

After the train returned to Buzzards Bay, Coholan said the trip served as a “catalyst…that fuels conversation.”

Fernandes, Vieira and Xiarhos also offered remarks supporting the effort and thanking Coholan for organizing the tour, with Xiarhos noting that everyone involved is “working together.”

Coholan concluded that the discussion has evolved beyond whether rail service should be extended to Buzzards Bay and is now focused on how to make it happen.

“There’s no reason why there can’t be a ‘next,’” he added, noting that the next phase is to “figure out what the ‘next’ is.”
