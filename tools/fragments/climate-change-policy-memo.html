Executive Summary

2023 has been a year characterized by extreme weather conditions, deviating far from the norm, with every part of the country economically and physically feeling the effects of climate change. Therefore, it is time to recognize that it is a bipartisan issue. Based on public opinion polls, recommendations include placing accountability on corporations and incentivizing the implementation of environment-friendly regulations, acknowledging how opinions within the Republican party vary on age and political ideology, and increasing outreach with constituents to improve public understanding of the topic.

Place the Focus on Corporations

The slogan ‘Reuse, Reduce, Recycle’ has been used to promote the individual’s role in protecting the environment. While it is important to advocate being aware of one’s part, it perpetuates the misconception that it is primarily the individual consumer’s responsibility. It overlooks the fact that “100 active fossil fuel producers are linked to 71% of global industrial greenhouse gasses.”1 The average American household emits just 48 metric tons CO2e a year,2 while the U.S. emits 6,340 million metric tons of CO2 a year.3

This sentiment is reflected as 69% of US adults believe that large businesses and corporations are doing too little to help reduce the effects of global climate change. 62% think that the energy industry does too little as well.

Almost the majority (48%) think that they are doing about the right amount to help reduce the effects of global climate change, but 66% believe that ordinary Americans are doing too little. This contradictory statement highlights how there is a tendency to be self-confident, yet concerned about the actions of others, or the perceived lack thereof.

Republicans Are More Diverse Than You Think…

While it can be easy to generalize political parties, it can be insightful to recognize the internal differences and nuances. This includes generational and ideological differences.

Generational Differences

Republican and Republican-leaning individuals showcase differences in their prioritization of significant lifestyle changes and efforts to reduce the effects of climate change for future generations. Around half of Republican and Republican-leaning Gen Z respondents say reducing climate change for the next generation is a top priority, compared to the 26% of Boomer and older generation respondents who think the same. Republican and Republican-leaning Gen Z respondents are also more likely than their older generations to believe that major lifestyle changes will be needed to address climate change.

Younger Republican and Republican-leaning voters show a preference for greener energy sources, differing from older generations who tend to favor drilling, hydraulic fracturing, and mining. This indicates that there is a shifting trend within the Republican Party, which over time may result in an increasing majority of Republicans who support legislation and other efforts to mitigate climate change and, in turn, protect the environment.

Ideological Differences

In today’s political climate, ideology has increasingly become synonymous with political party identification, significantly contributing to the escalating polarization of parties and their respective issues. By examining the ideological variation within the Republican party, a consistent distinction can be made between conservatives and those who identify themselves as moderate or liberal. There is a sentiment among the latter group that the federal government should do more to protect the quality of air and water, wildlife, ecosystems, and open lands within protected areas.

There is a discernible division among Republicans when it comes to addressing America’s energy supply. There is a clear split between those who support developing alternative energy and those who would prefer expanding fossil fuels. In 2019 and 2020, 62% and 65%, respectively, favored developing alternative energy sources, with the remaining 37% and 35% endorsing expanding fossil fuels. Republican voters can and will support climate-friendly efforts. While 52% of Republicans in 2021 favored expanding fossil fuels, the other 47% sponsored the development of alternative energy sources, signaling only a slight marginal difference.

Why Bipartisanship? Because Climate Change Does Not Care About Your Politics

Climate change impacts everyone. Recent weather conditions have been characterized by below- and above-average temperatures, extremely dry or wet conditions, intensified by flooding, tornadoes, and Nor’easters. By the end of April, “the U.S. was struck with seven separate weather and climate disasters, each with losses exceeding $1 billion,” surpassing the total cost of “$19 billion and resulted in 97 direct and indirect casualties.”4 These ethical and economic impacts of climate change must be recognized and addressed.

Incentivizing Is (Ever)green

There is a consensus for various solutions, with a majority calling for corporations to increase their efforts. 85% of US adults support requiring oil and gas companies to seal methane leaks from oil wells, with 70% endorsing taxing corporations based on their carbon emissions and 76% supporting providing a tax credit to businesses for developing carbon capture and storage. Introducing the application of incentives to the process of climate policymaking stimulates the development of climate-friendly regulations and legislation in the future.

These initiatives have majority support among Republicans, as 77% favor requiring oil and gas companies to seal methane gas leaks from oil wells, 67% support offering tax incentives for developing carbon capture and storage technologies, and 50% endorse taxing corporations based on their carbon emissions. Having corporations address their role and involvement in exacerbating climate change is a widely embraced initiative and promotes a proactive commitment to protecting the environment.
