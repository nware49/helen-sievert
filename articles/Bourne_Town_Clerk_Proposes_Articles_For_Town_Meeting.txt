Title: Bourne Town Clerk Proposes Articles For Town Meeting
Author: HELEN SIEVERT
Date: 2026-02-06T00:00:00-05:00
URL: https://www.capenews.net/bourne/news/bourne-town-clerk-proposes-articles-for-town-meeting/article_13f0ddec-d452-47c2-b14a-7a90cd143b0c.html

On Tuesday , February 3 , Town Clerk Barry H. Johnson presented two proposed articles to increase dog licensing and vital records fees for Annual Town Meeting to the Select Board. The articles, if adopted by the town, are estimated to generate $22,000 in the general fund for Fiscal Year 2027.

Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem had tasked department heads to see if any fees could potentially be adjusted. The town clerk’s office noticed that both the dog licensing and vital records fees had not been adjusted for a long time.

Johnson proposed increasing dog licensing fees from $7 to $10 for spayed female and neutered male dogs and from $10 to $15 for both male and female dogs, which should produce “$9,000 in extra revenue.” The dog licensing fee has not been adjusted since the October Town Meeting in 2005.

No changes were suggested to the March 31 licensing deadline or the $5 late fee. Licenses will continue to be enforced by any Bourne dog officer or police officer.

Regarding the vital records fees, Johnson proposes increasing birth, death and marriage certificates fees from $10 to $15. He also suggested increasing the marriage intentions fee from $25 to $40, which would also, if adopted by the town, include one certified copy of the marriage certificate. Based on the number of vital records requests, Johnson estimates that it would “generate approximately $13,000 in revenue for FY27.” The vital records fee has not been changed since the Annual May Town Meeting in 2011.

“I think they’re reasonable increases,” Johnson said of the proposed articles.

“They’re really not that much of an increase if you look at it overall,” Select Board chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti said, referencing that the changes were made around 15 and 21 years ago. Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian agreed.
