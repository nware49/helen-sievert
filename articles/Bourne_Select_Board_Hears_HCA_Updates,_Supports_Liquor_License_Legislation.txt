Title: Bourne Select Board Hears HCA Updates, Supports Liquor License Legislation

The Bourne Select Board at its meeting on June 30 received updates from the three cannabis dispensaries that were awarded a social equity host community agreement (HCA) with the town: Capeway Cannabis, Bourne Cannabis and Chief Cannabis.

As outlined in the town’s social equity policy, the purpose of the HCA was to encourage “diversity in the local cannabis industry.”

Kevin Hough of Capeway Cannabis announced that the store is open as of June 25. He shared that after organizing a career fair, the business was able to hire employees, 80% of whom are Bourne residents and 65% who are women.

“We’re up and running and doing great,” he added.

Michael Kinahan of Bourne Cannabis said the business has been “on schedule” and the goal is to open in April 2027. In the next couple of weeks, he said, the business will apply for a provisional license from the Cannabis Control Commission (CCC).

After discussions with the Board of Health in April and the Planning Board in May, Allen said, he received the architectural plans last week. Once the business has signed with a general contractor, the hope is to obtain a building permit in the coming weeks in order to begin construction, with the goal being completion in either December or January, he said.

Michael Allen of Chief Cannabis said he is anticipating opening in August. The business has received a provisional license from the CCC and completed its first inspection with the CCC, Allen said, with the hope of receiving the final license and inspection in July.

A general manager is “just about” on board, Allen said, adding that the business is organizing a career fair to be held in the next couple of weeks.

The Select Board voted unanimously to file special legislation for an annual all-alcoholic beverages retail license for off-premises consumption at 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset. A citizen’s petition filed by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H&J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms in Pocasset, had been approved at Special Town Meeting in May.

At the board’s last meeting on the matter on June 9 Patel’s attorney, Arthur M. Pearlman, had requested that the board file a petition with the Legislature for a special legislation liquor license.

Wanting to maintain consistency in regard to the transferability language the board had approved in the special legislation liquor license for Cataumet Fish, the board had directed Bertram to add that language to the draft provided by Pearlman.

Pearlman was provided a copy of the amended draft before last Tuesday’s meeting and proposed minor edits, which Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem confirmed were made.

Zack Fentross of CliftonLarsonAllen (CLA), the Town of Bourne’s independent audit firm, was at last Tuesday’s meeting to explain the Fiscal Year 2025 financial audit presentation to the Select Board..

The town received “an unmodified opinion,” which is “the best opinion you can receive from an independent auditor,” Fentross said, adding that this is “the same opinion that the town has received in prior years.”

Fentross said the town received a clean opinion on the single audit that was conducted on two programs–school lunch and special education. He added that this is “the best opinion you can receive.”

“All of your significant transactions are included in financial statements and they are posted in the proper period,” Fentross said, noting the statements were found “to be neutral, consistent and clear.” He added that the town had implemented two new accounting standards.

In regard to estimates, Fentross said CLA did observe that three estimates—other post-employment benefits (OPEB), incurred but not reported (IBNR) and landfill liability—had a “higher degree of uncertainty.”

“We didn’t identify any difficulties or we didn’t encounter any difficulties. There were no uncorrected misstatements and there were no disagreements with management throughout our audit process,” he added.

The unrestricted deficit net position of approximately $86 million is “nothing to be concerned about,” Fentross said while presenting the financial highlights. He said many other communities in the commonwealth also experience this due to net OPEB liability and pension liability.

The unassigned fund balance is $14.6 million, which Fentross described as the “most important figure in your financial statements.”

Noting that bond rating agencies compare a town’s “general unassigned fund balance to the expenditures and transfers out in your general fund,” Fentross said that Bourne’s ratio of 15.5% “is a good financial position” and “will be viewed favorably by users of the financial statements.”

The sewer enterprise fund had a net position of $7.4 million and the landfill enterprise fund sits at a total net position of $29.7 million, he said.

Fentross noted that the town’s debt associated with governmental activities and business activities decreased from the year prior due to “principal payments that were made on your outstanding debt.”

He explained that the town has a “funding percentage of 13.6%” for OPEB, which is a “good financial position” since other communities in the commonwealth are on average between 0% and 10%. “What users of financial statements also like to see is a commitment to making contributions to that OPEB trust fund year over year over year, which the Town of Bourne does do.”

As part of the Barnstable County Retirement Association, the town has a net pension liability of $43.1 million.

Pivoting to the management letter, Fentross recommended that the Finance Department continue to use an inventory tracking software to get the “rest of their equipment in inventory into that perpetual inventory tracking system.” He also provided information on upcoming accounting standards that focus on financial reporting models and management discussion and analysis.

The Select Board voted unanimously to approve a Fiscal Year 2026 operating budget year-end transfer of $143,527.74. In addition to approving the year-end transfer at its meeting on June 29, the Finance Committee had unanimously approved a transfer of $250,000 from the reserve fund, which it oversees.

“At the end of each year, we have some flexibility during the months of May and June and the first 15 days of the next fiscal year to transfer any appropriated funds from one appropriation to another,” town Finance Director Erica Flemming said. “This year we are requesting two appropriations transfers: one to supplement the snow and ice deficit and the other to supplement the fire overtime salaries.”

Select Board clerk Mary Jane Mastrangelo sought clarity about how the Fire Department’s overtime salary deficit would be covered. The deficit of $350,000 would be covered by the Finance Committee’s approval of the reserve fund transfer of $250,000, $50,000 from the Fire Department’s unused expenses and $50,000 from the year-end transfer request.

In a letter to the Select Board, Finance Committee and McCollem, Flemming explained that the money that the Fire Department had appropriated for its FY26 budget was not sufficient due to “unforeseen and necessary salary expenditures,” such as personnel overtime, resulting in the requests for reserve fund and year-end transfers.

The remaining year-end transfer amount of $93,527.74 would be used to cover the snow and ice deficit, which developed due to the severe weather from this past winter.

Town Engineer Timothy Lydon presented updates to the Select Board in regard to the two projects occurring at Hen Cove in Pocasset: the boat ramp and stormwater drainage.

Lydon said both projects are “substantially completed,” with limited or minor work remaining.

The Select Board voted unanimously to not exercise its right of first refusal to purchase a 0.97-acre portion of land after the property’s owners sent the town a notice of intent to sell, pursuant to Chapter 61A, Section 14 of the state’s general laws.

The land, which is located within the parcel at 665 Scenic Highway and 0 Bournedale Road, falls under that state statute through its agricultural use.

In the notice of intent to sell, the sellers, trustees of the Panhandle Trust, stated that the buyers, NextGrid Energy LLC, which is a renewable power company, propose to convert the land’s use from agricultural and horticultural to commercial, industrial or residential. The asking price for the land is $600,000.

At its last meeting on the matter on June 9, the board had decided to ask the Conservation Commission, Assessor’s Office, Planning Board and Open Space Committee to notify the Select Board whether they have interest in the town purchasing the land.

Responses to the board stated that there was no interest, resulting in the board’s vote to waive its opportunity to purchase the land.

The Select Board Board heard an update in regard to the redesign of the town seal from board chairwoman Melissa A. Ferretti and Historical Commission vice chairman Steven Philbrick.

After the board had voted to revise the town seal on February 21, 2024, the effort was paused. However, Ferretti said that she, Philbrick and Historical Commission member Jack MacDonald had resumed conversations on the matter.

Ferretti informed the board that she shared contact information of the advertising firm that redesigned the Town of Mashpee’s seal recently.

“Any redesign is going to need a clear plan, defined parties, strong community outreach so residents understand why the seal needs to be updated in the first place,” Ferretti added.

Noting that he had contacted the advertising firm that Ferretti had mentioned, Philbrick said the firm was “quite excited to help us if we wanted to use them.” The chief executive officer of the firm accepted an invitation to attend a Historical Commission meeting in August, Philbrick added.

The firm had noted that due to its experience in Mashpee, it has a rough understanding of costs and knowledge of funding sources and grants.

Philbrick noted that while the seal was drawn by renowned artist Charles Raleigh and “we don’t want to destroy the history there, but we want to make it historically accurate and something that reflects the vision of the town.”

Both Philbrick and Ferretti emphasized that the process will take time.

Ferretti told the Select Board that the Upper Cape Cod Regional Technical School (UCT) had established an advisory group, Cape Tech Subcommittee, to “help organize their response and improve communication.”

Ferretti noted that the Select Board had requested information from UCT in regard to “enrollment and the issue of the out-of-town students being counted in ways that affected our Bourne assessment,” which the subcommittee is “beginning to address.”

Citing board member Peter J. Meier’s suggestion to have more “round table discussions” with UCT, board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian proposed inviting the subcommittee to a meeting to have a conversation and “together get the answers that we need.”
