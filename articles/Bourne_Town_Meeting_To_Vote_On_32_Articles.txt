Title: Bourne Town Meeting To Vote On 32 Articles
Author: HELEN SIEVERT
Date: 2026-04-29T13:15:00-04:00
URL: https://www.capenews.net/bourne/news/bourne-town-meeting-to-vote-on-32-articles/article_b9d382c3-6679-4f0e-8c9c-8d1bdfebabd9.html

Bourne residents will have the chance to vote on 32 articles at the Annual and Special Town Meetings next week, with the annual warrant featuring 29 articles and the special warrant totaling three articles.

Both meetings are slated for Monday, May 4; the Annual Town Meeting will determine spending for the upcoming Fiscal Year 2027, and the Special Town Meeting will allow for adjustments to be made to the current FY26. Tuesday, May 5, has been designated as the second night of Town Meeting, if necessary.

The 29 articles on the annual warrant range from annual and boilerplate requests, operating budgets, fund appropriations, town hall certificate and document costs, capital outlay projects and Community Preservation Act-funded projects to road betterments, a road acceptance, purchasing a conservation restriction, zoning by-law amendments, easements of land regarding the relocation of gas pipelines and funding for a regional Housing Assistance Corporation project.

The Special Town Meeting warrant features three articles, two of which involve FY26 budgetary adjustments and the third being a citizen’s petition.

Articles 1 through 4 and 6 would require a simple majority to pass. Articles 5, 7 through 10 and 29 would require a two-thirds supermajority to pass. Articles 11 through 28 would require a majority to pass.

Article 1 is the consent agenda, which includes regular required authorizations, elected officials’ salaries, Chapter 90, revolving funds and accrued contractual compensated absences. The warrant explains that these items “recur annually and are housekeeping in nature.” The Select Board sponsored the article.

Article 2 requests the appropriation of $84,586,115 for the proposed Town of Bourne’s Fiscal Year 2027 operating budget, which is a 2.63% increase from FY26 and includes debt services costs and reflects the proposed adjustment to the FY26 snow and ice budget. The budget, constrained by service demands and shared costs, is now balanced following discussions and adjustments over the past couple of months.

Article 3 requests $1,949,450 for the FY27 Sewer Enterprise Fund. It also requests that “$181,283 be transferred to the General Fund to offset Sewer Enterprise indirect expenses and in order to meet this appropriation, we move that the sum of $2,030,733 be raised from Sewer Enterprise receipts and $100,000 be transferred from Sewer Enterprise retained earnings,” the warrant states.

Article 4 requests $15,380,924 for the FY27 Integrated Solid Waste Management Program (ISWM) Enterprise operating budget. The warrant also requests that “$2,784,942 be transferred to the General Fund to offset ISWM Enterprise indirect expenses, and in order to meet this appropriation, the sum of $18,165,866 is being proposed to be raised from ISWM Enterprise receipts.”

In Article 5 , the Select Board and the Capital Outlay Committee sponsored a number of requests totaling $2,961,350 to cover the costs of capital outlay projects. The Engineering Department requested $30,000 from free cash for GIS map digitization. The Shore and Harbor Department requested $250,000 from the Waterways Improvement Fund for annual dredging and repairs to ramps and piers.

The Police Department requested $275,000 from free cash to replace three vehicles and $163,000 from free cash for a new records management system. The Fire Department requested $275,000 from free cash to replace firefighters’ structural personal protection equipment and $85,000 from free cash to replace the fire prevention officer’s truck. Bourne Public Schools requested $150,000 from free cash for safety and security upgrades and $70,000 from free cash for window security.

The Department of Public Works requested $297,000 from free cash to design a new Singing Bridge in Pocasset. The Sewer Department requested $732,000 from enterprise borrowings for the Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) system to monitor the municipally owned sewer pump stations. ISWM requested $634,350 from enterprise retained earnings to replace a front-end loader.

Article 6 , which is sponsored by the Select Board, requests that Town Meeting “rescind the vote taken under Article 5-12 of the May 2024 Annual Town Meeting,” which authorized the Town to borrow funds for school wastewater treatment plant repairs. The scope of work on the wastewater treatment plant has changed, and different funds are being used, so the previously approved borrowing is no longer needed.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Articles 7 and 8 are related to the relocation of three Algonquin Gas Transmission pipelines, which the Massachusetts Department of Transportation asked Enbridge, the pipelines’ owner, to move due to upcoming construction for the bridge replacement project.

Enbridge is seeking approval to use 2.76 acres of town-owned, Conservation Commission-managed land for the project. Because of Article 97, Enbridge is required to provide replacement land for the aforementioned parcels, as approved by the Conservation Committee.

Through Article 7 , Enbridge requests that the Select Board be authorized to petition the state Legislature to file legislation that would allow the Town of Bourne and its Conservation Commission to “transfer, release and convey a 50-foot-wide permanent right-of-way and easement” to Algonquin Gas Transmission, LLC, a subsidiary of Enbridge.

Article 8 seeks to authorize the Select Board and Conservation Commission to transfer the parcels to Enbridge.

Town Administrator Marlene V. McCollem had previously explained that Article 7 is to “allow [Enbridge] to use the protected open space” and Article 8 is to give “[Enbridge] the right to use the easement.” Both the Finance Committee and the Select Board unanimously voted to recommend both articles.

Article 9 relates to the betterment and acceptance of Spinnaker Lane and Daniel Webster Lane as public ways; they are currently private ways. The town had received a petition from the homeowners on the roads.

Sponsored by the Select Board, the article would authorize the board to borrow $602,250 for the construction and repair of the roads to meet the town’s regulations. This cost would be paid by tax betterments assessed to the homeowners on the road.

Regarding the article’s benefit to the town, McCollem previously stated that if the roads were taken by the town, “they would count towards the town’s Chapter 90 calculation for aid for public roads.”

Article 10 , sponsored by the Select Board, will have the town vote to appropriate $1,038,373.70 for resurfacing and temporary repairs of Tara Terrace, Eldridge Street, and Eldridge Street Extension. Unlike the previous article, the roads would remain private and not become public roads. The town had also received a petition from homeowners on the roads. The sum will be repaid through betterments assessed to the homeowners on the roads.

Article 11 , sponsored by the Select Board, will allow committees and town or state officers to share reports, recommendations, and updates with Town Meeting.

Articles 12 through 20 are funded through the Community Preservation Act and sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee. The funds can be spent on several types of projects, including affordable housing, historic preservation, and open space/recreation.

The Recreation Department requested $400,000 in Article 12 for the design and construction of the Cataumet Schoolhouse hardcourt project, which would feature a new post-tension concrete court with a 20-year guarantee and fencing to address disrepair, as well as improvements to make the parking, path, and court compliant with the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA).

The Recreation Department requested $350,000 in Article 13 for the Bourne Veterans’ Memorial Community Center shade structures project to provide two 15-foot cantilevered shade structures and ADA-compliant seating and walkways.

The Facilities Department requested $77,0000 in Article 14 to “fund the design costs associated with repairing the exterior envelope of Town Hall, including, but not limited to, design of masonry and window repairs to prevent water infiltration.”

The School Department requested $30,000 in Article 15 for a feasibility study of the central school administration building at 36 Sandwich Road to address a number of repairs.

Article 16 seeks to have the town vote to authorize the Select Board to purchase a conservation restriction for $325,000 for 940 County Road, a vacant wooded lot in Pocasset, as well as $10,000 for “incidental” costs.

Bourne Conservation Trust (BCT), a private nonprofit group, had recently bought the land for $650,000 for conservation purposes and proposed a partnership with the Town of Bourne, under which CPA funds would be used to purchase a conservation restriction on the property, preserving it in perpetuity. Per the CPA, open space parcels must be owned by the town for conservation or recreational purposes or be owned by a nonprofit.

The Housing Assistance Corporation (HAC) requests $100,000 in Article 17 for the renovation of 460 West Main Street in Hyannis to house the Assistance Resource Shelter, Cape Cod’s only state-licensed emergency overnight individual shelter. HAC is seeking $100,000 from every Cape town.

The Bourne Affordable Housing Trust requests $111,500 in Article 18 to support its FY27 budget and affordable housing services. The partnership is solely financed through CPA funds. The funds would assist with purchasing housing, support legal fees, and support continued monitoring by HAC.

The Community Preservation Committee requests $75,000 in Article 19 for the CPC’s administrative and operating expenses for Fiscal Year 2027. The funds would cover administrative services and costs, with the remainder going back to the Community Preservation Funds.

Article 20 , sponsored by the Community Preservation Committee, requests that the town appropriate $1,931,502.05 from FY27 estimated CPA revenues for the Community Preservation Fund Special Purpose Reserves. The article recommended that $1,502,279.37 would be for the reserve for open space; $214,611.34 would be for the reserve for community housing; and $214,611.34 for the reserve for historic preservation.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 21 requests $160,000 of free cash be moved into the Stabilization Fund, which McCollem had previously referred to as “the town’s rainy day account.”

Article 22 requests that $850,000 in free cash be transferred to the capital stabilization fund. McCollem had previously explained that the article aims to replenish the capital stabilization fund after “over a million and a half” was allocated at the Special Town Meeting in January 2025 to purchase 171 Clay Pond Road for the south side fire station.

Article 23 , sponsored by the Select Board, is a request by the Human Services Committee to transfer $40,000 from free cash to fund the human services grant program for FY27, which funds various human service agencies in town.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 24 would have Town Meeting vote to increase the demand fees assessed on unpaid taxes on bills, such as mooring and marina, sewer charges, boat excise, motor vehicle excise and property taxes, from $15 to $30.

Article 25 , sponsored by the Select Board, would increase dog licensing fees from $7 to $10 for spayed female and neutered male dogs and from $10 to $15 for both male and female dogs. The dog licensing fee has not been adjusted since the October Town Meeting in 2005.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 26 would increase vital records fees. Birth, death, and marriage certificate fees would increase from $10 to $15. And marriage intentions fees would increase from $25 to $40. The vital records fee has not been changed since the Annual May Town Meeting in 2011.

Article 27 , sponsored by the School Committee, requests that $115,962 be raised and appropriated to the Special Education Reserve Fund. This amount was “the savings from the Upper Cape Tech adjustment,” Finance Director Erica Flemming stated previously. “We’re putting that directly towards the Special Education Reserve Fund.”

Article 28 , sponsored by the Planning Board, would amend the Town of Bourne’s accessory dwelling unit (ADU) zoning bylaw to align it with state regulations. The proposed changes include allowing ADUs in downtown neighborhood districts, changing the required number of parking spaces for ADUs, and removing lot size and owner-occupancy requirements.

Article 29 , which is also sponsored by the Planning Board, would amend the Town of Bourne’s zoning bylaw to increase the maximum front setback from 15 feet to 30 feet for parcels that are located in the downtown district, but not on a primary street.

The Special Town Meeting was primarily called to allow a vote on a budget adjustment for FY26. A citizen’s petition was also included on the special warrant.

Article 1 , which is sponsored by the Select Board, requests $13,334.18 to pay unpaid bills from the Fiscal Year 2025, including a transfer of $11,675.38 from free cash to pay a missed encumbrance of one month’s legal bills, and $1,658.80 from ISWM retained earnings to pay for unpaid ISWM bills. The article would require a nine-tenths supermajority to pass.

Sponsored by the Select Board, Article 2 requests to appropriate $470,000 in free cash to Public Works. This would help cover the $800,000 deficit in the FY26 snow and ice budget. A simple majority would be required for the article to pass.

This article would offset the costs from this past winter by spending down reserves and using the free cash that had previously funded two articles that had been featured on the upcoming Annual Town Meeting warrant before having been removed and deferred to the Fall Town Meeting, McCollem said on April 1, adding that the departments would “cover the rest of the deficit with year-end transfers at the end of the fiscal year.”

While the town is allowed to deficit-spend in the snow and ice budget and raise the budget on the recap sheet, this would be at a cost to taxpayers.

Article 3 is a petition by Jigneshkumar Patel, an owner of H & J Convenience Inc., which also does business as Country Farms, for the town to apply for an annual all alcoholic beverages retail license. The business is located at 365 Barlows Landing Road in Pocasset. A simple majority would be required for the petition to pass.

Town Counsel Bryan F. Bertram previously stated that the license Patel is applying for is not available because it has already been granted. He added that the article can be included at the Special Town Meeting, but since the Select Board is the licensing authority, the Town Meeting cannot grant a liquor license.

The applicant applied for a liquor license by filing a citizen’s petition instead of using the standard application process. Since a citizen’s petition can only be withdrawn by the petitioner themselves, Bertram said he would explain the liquor license application process to the petitioner.

Having provided their recommendations for all of the other articles on the annual and special warrants, the Finance Committee and Select Board will provide their verbal recommendation for article 3 on the special warrant at the night of the Town Meeting.

Town Meeting will be held on May 4 at 7 PM at the Bourne High School auditorium. More information about both warrants can be found at bourne-ma.gov/1313/Annual-Special-Town-Meeting---May-4-2026.
