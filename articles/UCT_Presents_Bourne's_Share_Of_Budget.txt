Title: UCT Presents Bourne's Share Of Budget
Author: HELEN SIEVERT
Date: 2026-04-03T00:00:00-04:00
URL: https://www.capenews.net/bourne/news/uct-presents-bournes-share-of-budget/article_f24be00e-ffa0-4ce9-b509-41143132f0a6.html

Upper Cape Cod Regional Technical School (UCT) Superintendent Roger Forget presented the school’s Fiscal Year 2027 operating budget to the Bourne Select Board and Finance Committee on Monday , March 30 . Bourne will provide $4,322,089 to UCT’s overall budget. This share increased $230,971, or 5.65%, over FY26.

Enrollment has continued to increase over the years, Forget noted. In the 2019-20 school year, 742 students were enrolled. During this school year, 2025-26, 892 students attend UCT, including those enrolled in the licensed practical nurse (LPN) program. A total of 878 UCT students are enrolled in grades 9 through 12.

Of the 218 UCT students who live in Bourne, 215 are enrolled in 9th through 12th grades, with the remaining three students enrolled in the LPN program.

In addition to the increase in student enrollment, Forget said that vocational supply costs, contractual obligations and insurance increases had been the main pressure points to UCT’s FY27 gross foundation budget of $21,379,762.

Since UCT “acts as a municipality,” he said, “we have components that we are responsible for and are part of our budget that are not part of our school system,” noting OPEP, employee retirement, retiree health care and snow removal.

Forget presented a breakdown of the gross total budget across various categories. The administration budget—which includes UCT’s School Committee, superintendent’s office and business and finance costs—is allocated $1,040,361, or 4.9% of the budget. That is an increase of 5.38% over FY26.

The majority of the budget, $12,879,782, or 60.2%, is allocated to the instruction budget. The 8.27% increase over FY26 is due primarily to contractual obligations for teachers’ salaries, three new teaching positions—a math teacher, a special education teacher and an English teacher—and an increase in instructional technology to support the incoming freshman class.

The student services budget is apportioned $998,885, or 4.7% of the budget. Contractual obligations for UCT’s two school nurses led to the 3.08% increase over FY26.

The operations and maintenance budget is set at $2,078,620, representing 9.7% of the budget. “Minor increases” resulted in the budget increasing 1.31% compared to FY26.

The fixed charges budget—which includes employee benefits, the insurance program and School Choice—is apportioned $4,382,113, or 20.5% of the budget. The main source of the 2.82% increase over FY26 is an increase in building insurance.

UCT does not participate in School Choice, Forget noted. “You have a student from Bourne going to Falmouth…it comes into our budget and it goes back out.”

Select Board member Mary Jane Mastrangelo inquired about the $71,000 that is budgeted for School Choice. Based on the enrollment number, Forget said, the state Department of Revenue provides that number. “They’re using this as a mechanism for how you fund a Chapter 74 student from one town to another town,” he said. “It comes in and goes right back out and it is not part of our actual budget.”

“I do not understand that,” Mastrangelo said. Select Board vice chairwoman Anne-Marie Siroonian concurred.

Bourne Public Schools superintendent Kerri Anne Quinlan-Zhou provided a hypothetical example that if a Bourne resident is enrolled in Falmouth Public Schools and is part of a Chapter 74 vocational program there, “the state sends [monies] to UCT to send to Falmouth for the Bourne student who is in that program.”

The cost increased per student, Forget added.

To help offset the increased costs, Forget cited various revenue sources, including Chapter 70 aid, anticipated interest revenue, anticipated municipal Medicaid, and applied excess and deficiency. The total anticipated revenue is budgeted at $5,226,960.

Referencing the $80,000 in anticipated interest revenue, Finance Committee member Arthur Wayne Sampson asked which fund is paying that amount. Forget said interest revenue is produced from funds received by UCT. “That all goes into the bank,” he added. “It’s sitting in the account. It is generating interest.”

Revenue increased 11.82% from FY26 to FY27, as excess and deficiency “equal to free cash” increased to $350,000 and Chapter 70 aid increased to $4,786,960, Forget said.

After applying the revenue to the gross total budget, the net foundational budget came out to $16,152,802. Forget explained that the required net school spending is “the requirement by the state of what each town should pay [of] their foundation budget. That is the minimum that is required by the town to fund its school system.”

The state Department of Elementary and Secondary Education has set Bourne’s minimum contribution at $4,123,287. The overall remaining net foundational budget is $494,631, Forget said. “The total number of students, which is 215, divided by the total number of students [not in the LPN program] in the school gives you [.24487472]. That is what is multiplied by that $494,631 to give you the remainder of net to each town,” adding that in regard to Bourne, “Upper Cape Tech is $121,123 over foundation.”

While there were increases to the gross costs of transportation provided by the district and the LPN program, both costs were partially offset by state aid and tuition. For FY27, the net transportation budget is $111,886, while the net LPN program costs $2,901. The combined net total for transportation and LPN was $108,985, a decrease of 49.56% from FY26.

In FY27, capital is budgeted to increase to $330,000, an 8.2% increase over the prior fiscal year. Forget added that UCT is hoping to eventually hire an operating project manager to “just begin to look at a new roof on the school, a new gymnasium...and a small addition for new science lab areas.” He noted that since the school was built for 500 students, the current gymnasium can no longer house all of UCT’s enrolled students.

Prefacing that it is their role as committee and board members, Sampson and Siroonian said they have to explain to Bourne taxpayers why UCT’s budget assessment is increasing 5.56%, whereas the town departments’ budget is increasing 2.88%. Forget reiterated that contractual obligations, as well as an increase in building insurance, have increased the school’s budget.

Finance Committee vice chairwoman Amanda Bongiovanni asked about what is being done to control those costs and whether the UCT School Committee discussed potential cuts without impacting students in light of rising costs for everyone. Forget said the committee applied $350,000 of excess and deficiency to offset the gross budget total.

Mastrangelo noted how the increased enrollment size increased the assessment. She added that the regional budget is difficult due to the “many different pieces” and the “overhead costs” associated in the “budget drivers.”
